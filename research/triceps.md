# Triceps knowledge base — "How to train your muscles" v1

Research date: 25 Oct 2026. Conventions as in `biceps.md` and `chest-research.md`:
**[DISAGREEMENT]**, **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**, **[OUR THRESHOLD]** (checker cut-off we chose).
- **Elbow angle** = degrees of flexion, 0° = straight. A triceps rep goes from bent to ~0-5°.
- **Upper-arm angle** = humerus vs torso long axis, sagittal plane; 0° = hanging, +180° = straight
  overhead, negative = behind the body.
- **Elbow flare** (close-grip bench, dips) as defined in the chest file: humerus projected on the
  thorax coronal plane vs the caudal axis, 0° = elbow on the ribs, 90° = "T".
- Tempo default 2-0-1-1. Beginner default 3 x 8-12, 2-3 reps in reserve, 2 sessions/week.

## 1. Anatomy (short)

| Head | Everyday name | Origin → insertion | What it does | Position that biases it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Long head** | "Inner/back of the arm", the big head that hangs under the arm | **Infraglenoid tubercle** of the scapula (blends with the shoulder capsule) → common tendon on the **olecranon** of the ulna | Elbow extension **plus** shoulder extension and adduction (it crosses two joints) | **Shoulder flexed / arm overhead** stretches it. Training overhead grew it 28.5% vs 19.6% at the side (Maeo 2023). Note: Kholinne 2018 found its *force share* highest at 0° shoulder elevation and the medial head's highest at ≥90° — length and force share are different things **[DISAGREEMENT]** |
| **Lateral head** | "Outer horseshoe" | Posterior humerus **above** the radial groove + lateral intermuscular septum → olecranon | Elbow extension only | Any elbow extension with the arm at the side or behind (pushdown, kickback). No study isolates it further; ACE 2011 found its EMG significantly *lower* than the long head's in pushdowns and lying extensions |
| **Medial head** | "Deep head" (mostly covered by the other two) | Broad origin on the posterior humerus **below** the radial groove + both septa → olecranon | Elbow extension; the "workhorse" active at all loads **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**, supported by Kholinne 2018 (highest force share at ≥90° elevation) and Akima 2021 (its EMG-force relation is the one most sensitive to elbow angle) | Every triceps exercise, especially the last 30° of lockout **[COACHING CONSENSUS]** |

Innervation: radial nerve (C6-C8). Sources: Kenhub (https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/triceps-brachii-muscle-en), Radiopaedia (https://radiopaedia.org/articles/triceps-brachii), StatPearls (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK536996/).

Anconeus (small, elbow-side) assists extension; show it only in the anatomy layer as a stabiliser.

Practical implication: all three heads extend the elbow in every exercise; only the **long head**
can be biased, by shoulder position (overhead = stretched; behind the body = shortened). Say
"bias", never "isolate".

angles**, or **kickback shoulder angles**. Those are flagged **[COACHING CONSENSUS]** below.

## 3. The exercises (6)

### 3.1 `cg_bench` — Close-grip bench press
- **Station:** flat bench with rack, barbell (20 kg), spotter or safety arms.
- **Parts:** Main: triceps (all heads; lateral + medial do the lockout, long head helps),
  pec major (sternal), anterior deltoid. Evidence: Saeterbakken 2021, Lehman 2005, Barnett 1995
  (narrower grip → more triceps); ACE 2011 (lowest triceps EMG at 70% 1RM — it is the *heavy*
  triceps lift, not the most "activating").
- **Real-world flow:** Walk to the bench, load plates, lie down with eyes under the bar, five
  points of contact. Grip at **shoulder width (1.0x biacromial, hands 35-45 cm apart)** — not
  touching hands. Unrack with straight arms (spotter lift-off for heavy sets), bar over the
  shoulders. Finish: press to lockout, move the bar back over the hooks, lower onto the J-hooks,
  let go, sit up, swing the legs off, stand. Wipe the bench.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Grip 1.0x biacromial; forearms vertical at the bottom from the foot-of-bench view.
  - Elbow flare **20-35°** at the bottom (arrow, tighter than the chest press's 45-60°).
  - Bar touches the lower sternum / upper abdomen (2-5 cm lower than the chest-press touch point).
  - Elbow angle: ~180° (lockout, we write "0° flexion" = straight) → **90-100° flexion** at the
    bottom → straight. Bar path nearly vertical with a slight back-drift to over the shoulders.
  - Scapulae retracted; modest thoracic arch; buttocks on the bench. Tempo 2-0-1-0.
- **Steps:** 1. Hands shoulder width, unrack. 2. Lower to lower chest. 3. Elbows tucked, press.
  4. Lock out over shoulders.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Grip too narrow* — hands < 25 cm apart (touching the knurling gap), wrists bent back
     20-30°, elbows flare to compensate. Metric: **inter-hand distance**: good 35-45 cm, bad ≤ 25
     cm **[OUR THRESHOLD]** (Saeterbakken: no triceps gain for narrow over medium). Now: wrist and
     elbow strain, less weight. Later: wrist/elbow tendon pain. Cue: "Shoulder width, not thumbs."
  2. *Elbows flare to 60-90°* — bar drifts to the nipple line, it becomes a wide bench. Metric:
     **elbow flare at the bottom**: good ≤ 35°, bad ≥ 60°. Now: pec and delt take the load.
     Later: shoulder stress; triceps undertrained. Cue: "Elbows brush the ribs."
  3. *Hips rise off the bench* — buttocks lift 5-10 cm at the sticking point. Metric: **pelvis-
     to-bench distance**: good ≤ 1 cm, bad ≥ 5 cm. Now: shorter ROM, lumbar extension under load.
     Later: lower-back strain. Cue: "Butt down, drive the floor."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 6-10, empty bar first; add 2.5 kg a side when 10 reps are clean.
- **Safety:** collars on; safety arms or a spotter; never to failure alone.

### 3.2 `rope_pushdown` — Cable rope pushdown
- **Station:** dual cable tower, pulley at the **top**, rope attachment.
- **Parts:** Main: triceps — lateral and medial heads do the work with the shoulder neutral; the
  long head is still active (ACE 2011: long 81% vs lateral 67% in rope pushdowns — the "pushdown =
  lateral head" claim is **[DISAGREEMENT]** with the ACE data; Kholinne 2018 also found the long
  head's force share highest at 0° shoulder). Helper: anconeus. Stabiliser: lats, core, forearm.
  ACE 2011 combined score 74% of triangle push-up.
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pulley to the top, clip the rope on, set the pin (beginners
  10-20 kg). Face the tower, take one rope end in each hand (thumbs up, neutral), step back half a
  step, pull the elbows to the ribs with the hands at chest height. Finish: let the rope rise
  under control to chest height, step in so the stack settles, let go, unclip and hang the rope.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Stance: feet hip-width or one foot slightly forward; torso pitch **10-15° forward**, chest up.
  - Upper arm fixed at **0 to +10°** (elbows just in front of the ribs); elbow stays 2-5 cm from
    the ribs for the whole rep.
  - Elbow: start **90-100° flexion** (hands at the lower chest) → **0-5°** at the bottom.
  - Forearm neutral; at the bottom the hands part 20-30 cm and rotate so the palms face back
    (the rope "split"), which adds the last 10° of extension.
  - Hand path: an arc centred on the elbow, finishing at the front of the thighs. Tempo 2-0-1-1.
- **Steps:** 1. Elbows to the ribs. 2. Push rope to thighs. 3. Split, lock out.
  4. Return to chest height, slowly.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Elbows swing* — elbows travel 15-20 cm forward then back, upper arm reaching +30-45° at the
     top. Metric: **upper-arm angle range in the rep**: good ≤ 10°, bad ≥ 30°. Now: lats and
     shoulder press the stack; triceps leverage drops. Later: progress stalls, elbow irritation.
     Cue: "Elbows glued, forearm hinge."
  2. *Leaning on the stack* — torso pitches forward 30°+ and bobs with every rep. Metric:
     **torso pitch range**: good ≤ 5°, bad ≥ 15° (or static pitch ≥ 30°). Now: bodyweight pushes
     the weight. Later: too heavy, no arm growth. Cue: "Lighter, stand tall."
  3. *Half lockout* — elbow stops at ≥ 30° flexion. Metric: **min elbow flexion**: good ≤ 5°, bad
     ≥ 30°. Now: medial head's last 30° never loaded. Later: weaker lockouts. Cue: "Straight arms,
     squeeze."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 12-15; the best first triceps exercise (no load to balance).
- **Safety:** step in before releasing the rope; keep the wrists straight.

### 3.3 `overhead_rope` — Overhead cable rope extension
- **Station:** dual cable tower, pulley at the **lowest** setting (Maeo 2023's overhead version was
  a low pulley), rope attachment; the lifter faces away from the tower.
- **Parts:** Main: triceps **long head** (stretched: shoulder flexed ~160-180°) — Maeo 2023:
  +28.5% long-head volume vs +19.6% at the side; whole triceps +19.9% vs +13.9%. Helper: lateral
  and medial heads (ACE 2011: overhead extension 76% combined, long 81, lateral 72). Stabiliser:
  core, serratus (holding the arms up).
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pulley to the bottom, clip the rope. Face the tower, pick the rope
  up with both hands, turn 180° so the back is to the tower, step forward into a split stance
  (one foot 40-60 cm ahead), and in one motion bring the rope over the head so the hands are
  behind the neck, elbows pointing forward-up. Finish: bend the elbows to the start, bring the
  hands down in front of the shoulders, turn to face the tower, lower the rope to the stack, let
  go, unclip.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Split stance, torso pitch **10-20° forward**, ribs down (no lumbar arch), chin neutral.
  - Upper arm fixed at **+160° to +180°** (beside the ears), elbows 15-20 cm apart, pointing
    forward and up; the cable runs past the hip and up the back.
  - Elbow: start **100-120° flexion** (hands behind the head, rope at the nape) → **0-10°**
    overhead; forearm neutral; hands may separate 10-15 cm at lockout.
  - Hand path: an arc from behind the head to straight above-forward of the head. Tempo
    **3-1-1-0** (slow 3 s lowering into the stretch, 1 s pause — the stretch is the exercise).
- **Steps:** 1. Back to tower, rope behind head. 2. Elbows up beside ears. 3. Extend overhead.
  4. Lower slowly into the stretch.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Elbows flare wide* — elbows drift from 15-20 cm apart to 40-50 cm and point sideways
     (shoulders externally rotate). Metric: **inter-elbow distance at the bottom**: good ≤ 25 cm,
     bad ≥ 40 cm. Now: shoulders and traps share the load. Later: shoulder irritation, less
     long-head stretch. Cue: "Elbows narrow, point them forward."
  2. *Lower-back arch* — lumbar extension 20°+, ribs flare, belly forward as the weight goes up.
     Metric: **pelvis-to-thorax angle change**: good ≤ 5°, bad ≥ 15°. Now: the back braces for
     the arms. Later: lumbar strain. Cue: "Ribs down, squeeze the glutes."
  3. *Upper arms drop forward* — the upper arm falls to +120° so the hands move in front of the
     head (becomes a front-press). Metric: **upper-arm angle at the bottom**: good ≥ 155°, bad
     ≤ 125°. Now: long head loses its stretch. Later: no overhead benefit (Maeo). Cue: "Biceps
     touch the ears."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-15, light (5-15 kg): Maeo's subjects used lower loads overhead than at the
  side; that is expected.
- **Safety:** shoulder impingement or limited overhead mobility → use a 60° incline bench and
  a single dumbbell instead (upper arm at +120-140°).

### 3.4 `skull_crusher` — Lying EZ-bar skull crusher
- **Station:** flat bench (no rack needed), EZ bar from the rack.
- **Parts:** Main: triceps, all heads; **long head** gets more stretch the further the upper arm
  is angled back toward the head **[COACHING CONSENSUS]** (no EMG by arm angle found). Helper:
  anconeus. Stabiliser: anterior deltoid (holds the arm), forearm. ACE 2011: lying barbell
  extension scored 62% combined (long 70, lateral 55) at 70% 1RM.
- **Real-world flow:** Take the EZ bar (plus small plates) from the rack with a narrow pronated
  grip on the inner angles, carry it to the flat bench, sit on the end with the bar on the thighs,
  lie back and press the bar to straight arms over the chest, then angle the arms 10-15° back
  toward the head. Finish: lower the bar to the chest (bend the elbows and drop the upper arms),
  sit up with the bar on the thighs, stand, rack the bar.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Lying, five points of contact. Grip pronated, hands 20-25 cm apart on the EZ angles.
  - Upper arm fixed at **+100° to +110°** relative to the torso (angled back toward the head;
    vertical = +90° would unload the top). It does not move during the rep.
  - Elbow: **0-5°** at the top → **100-120° flexion** at the bottom; the bar passes just over
    the forehead/hairline and finishes **behind** the head at the bottom (5-10 cm above the bench
    behind the crown), not on the forehead.
  - Forearm pronated; wrists straight; elbows 20-25 cm apart, pointing to the ceiling.
  - Bar path: an arc centred on the elbows. Tempo 3-0-1-0 (slow lowering; this is the
    "skull" part).
- **Steps:** 1. Bar over the face, arms back. 2. Bend elbows, bar behind head. 3. Elbows still,
  extend. 4. Lock out, arms angled back.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Elbows flare out* — elbows spread from 22 cm to 40 cm+ and point outward. Metric:
     **inter-elbow distance at the bottom**: good ≤ 25 cm, bad ≥ 40 cm. Now: shoulders and chest
     join; elbows take a sideways load. Later: elbow tendon pain (the skull-crusher complaint).
     Cue: "Elbows in, point them up."
  2. *Upper arms rock* — the upper arm swings from +110° to +70° and back each rep (a "pullover
     press"). Metric: **upper-arm angle range**: good ≤ 10°, bad ≥ 30°. Now: lats and shoulders
     move the bar. Later: triceps undertrained. Cue: "Freeze the upper arm."
  3. *Bar to the forehead, half range* — bar stops 5 cm above the forehead with the elbow at 70°.
     Metric: **max elbow flexion**: good ≥ 100°, bad ≤ 70°. Now: skips the stretched range.
     Later: less growth; a real forehead strike risk. Cue: "Past the head, not onto it."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-12 with the empty EZ bar (≈ 10 kg) or +2.5 kg a side.
- **Safety:** never to failure without a spotter; keep the bar's path behind the head; if the
  elbows hurt, use the overhead rope instead.

### 3.5 `dip_upright` — Upright triceps dip (parallel bars)
- **Station:** dip station (bars shoulder-width or slightly wider).
- **Parts:** Main: triceps (all heads), pec major (sternocostal). Helper: anterior deltoid.
  Stabiliser: serratus, lats, core. Evidence: McKenzie 2022 (bar dips: triceps and pec are the
  agonists, more than bench dips). ACE 2011 "dips" scored 87% but those were **bench dips** with
  the hands behind the body (flagged there for shoulder stress). The upright-torso/elbows-back
  = "triceps dip", lean-forward = "chest dip" split is **[COACHING CONSENSUS]** (no study found).
- **Real-world flow:** Walk to the dip station, grip the handles, jump or step up to straight
  arms (feet off the step), cross the ankles behind, keep the torso vertical, shoulders down.
  Finish: lower to the bottom, uncross, put the feet on the step/floor, let go. Beginners: the
  gym's mats under the station for a box-assisted version.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Torso pitch **0-10° forward** (vs 15-30° for the chest dip); hips under the shoulders; knees
    bent 90°, ankles crossed behind.
  - Elbow flare **≤ 20°** (elbows point straight back); forearms vertical.
  - Elbow: **0-5°** at the top → **~90° flexion** at the bottom (upper arm about parallel to the
    floor). Shoulder goes from 0° to about -40 to -50° (extension behind the body).
  - Scapulae depressed at the top (no shrug), allowed to elevate slightly at the bottom.
  - Tempo 2-0-1-0.
- **Steps:** 1. Jump to straight arms. 2. Stay upright, bend elbows. 3. Upper arm parallel.
  4. Press back, lock out.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Elbows flare* — flare reaches 45-60° and the elbows point sideways. Metric: **elbow flare at
     the bottom**: good ≤ 20°, bad ≥ 45°. Now: it becomes a chest dip with extra shoulder load.
     Later: front-shoulder strain. Cue: "Elbows point behind you."
  2. *Too deep* — shoulder joint drops below the elbow (upper arm > 10° below horizontal), the
     shoulder rolls forward. Metric: **upper-arm angle below horizontal at the bottom**: good
     ≤ 5°, bad ≥ 15°. Now: anterior shoulder capsule loaded at end range. Later: shoulder
     impingement/instability. Cue: "Stop at parallel."
  3. *Shrugging and swinging* — shoulders rise 5 cm toward the ears, legs swing 20 cm+. Metric:
     **ankle horizontal travel per rep**: good ≤ 5 cm, bad ≥ 20 cm. Now: momentum. Later: fewer
     effective reps, neck strain. Cue: "Quiet legs, shoulders down."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 5-8 (or 3 x 4 slow negatives / box-assisted); weighted belt later.
- **Safety:** AC-joint or front-shoulder pain → replace with the close-grip bench or pushdown.

### 3.6 `kickback` — Dumbbell kickback
- **Station:** dumbbell rack + flat bench (one hand and knee on the bench) or standing hinged.
- **Parts:** Main: triceps, all heads; the long head is **shortened** at both ends at the top
  (shoulder extended + elbow straight), so the peak contraction is the point **[COACHING
  CONSENSUS]**. ACE 2011: kickbacks 87% combined (long 88, lateral 87), statistically equal to the
  triangle push-up and bench dip, i.e. the highest-scoring free-weight move in that study at
  70% 1RM. Stabiliser: posterior deltoid (holds the arm up), core.
- **Real-world flow:** Take one light dumbbell from the rack, walk to the flat bench, put the
  same-side knee and hand on the bench (or stand in a split stance with the free hand on the
  bench), hinge the torso to 10-20° above horizontal, raise the upper arm until it is in line with
  the torso (elbow by the ribs, pointing back), forearm hanging down. Finish: lower the forearm,
  stand up, swap arms, then return the dumbbell to the rack.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Torso pitch **70-80° forward** (10-20° above horizontal), back flat, head neutral.
  - Upper arm fixed at **-10° to 0°** relative to the torso, i.e. parallel to the floor or just
    above it; elbow 2-5 cm from the ribs. Zero upper-arm movement during the rep.
  - Elbow: start **90° flexion** (forearm vertical) → **0-5°** (whole arm one straight line,
    dumbbell behind the hip, higher than the torso line is not required).
  - Forearm neutral (thumb forward); wrist straight.
  - Hand path: a quarter-circle backward and up, radius = forearm length. Tempo 1-1-1-0 (1 s
    squeeze at lockout is the exercise).
- **Steps:** 1. Hinge, upper arm level. 2. Kick the forearm back. 3. Squeeze, arm straight.
  4. Lower the forearm only.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Upper arm drops* — the elbow sags 15-25 cm below the torso line (upper arm at -45° to -60°,
     pointing at the floor). Metric: **upper-arm angle vs the torso at the top**: good ≥ -10°,
     bad ≤ -40°. Now: gravity no longer loads the lockout; the rep gets easy. Later: no stimulus.
     Cue: "Elbow higher than your back."
  2. *Swinging the upper arm* — the upper arm swings 20-30° per rep (a rowing motion). Metric:
     **upper-arm angle range**: good ≤ 5°, bad ≥ 20°. Now: rear delt and lats do a half-row.
     Later: nothing for the triceps. Cue: "Only the forearm moves."
  3. *Half lockout* — elbow stops at ≥ 30° flexion. Metric: **min elbow flexion**: good ≤ 5°, bad
     ≥ 30°. Now: the top squeeze, which is the whole exercise, never happens. Later: wasted sets.
     Cue: "Straight arm, one second."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 12-15 per arm with 2-6 kg; it is a light exercise by design.
- **Safety:** keep the back flat and the neck neutral; light dumbbells only.

## 4. Today's plan (triceps)

**Plan A — "Heavy press, then the long head" (11 hard sets)**
| # | Exercise | Sets x reps | Rest | Why (part) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Close-grip bench press | 3 x 6-10 | 2-3 min | Heaviest load for all three heads; compound first (Simão 2012); 3 min rest for strength (Schoenfeld 2016) |
| 2 | Overhead cable rope extension | 3 x 10-15 | 90 s | **Long head** stretched — 1.4x the growth of arm-at-side extensions (Maeo 2023) |
| 3 | Cable rope pushdown | 3 x 12-15 | 60-75 s | Lateral + medial heads with the shoulder neutral; full lockout for the medial head |
| 4 | Upright dip | 2 x 5-8 | 90 s | All heads under bodyweight; lockout strength |

**Plan B — "Free weights only" (11 hard sets)**
| # | Exercise | Sets x reps | Rest | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Upright dip | 3 x 5-8 | 2 min | Compound first; all heads |
| 2 | Lying EZ-bar skull crusher | 3 x 10-12 | 90 s | Long head stretched with the arms angled back (coaching consensus); heavy isolation |
| 3 | Dumbbell kickback | 3 x 12-15 / arm | 60 s | Long head shortened, peak-contraction finisher (ACE 2011: 87%) |
| 4 | Cable rope pushdown | 2 x 15 | 60 s | Lateral/medial pump, full lockout |

Weekly 10-20 hard sets per muscle (see chest §4); every press already counts as fractional triceps
work, so 2 triceps sessions of ~10 sets with a chest day is already at the upper end.

## 6. Changelog and open questions

Changelog (25 Oct 2026, first pass): file created. ACE 2011 Table 1 copied from the ACE article
with per-head values; "dips" there identified as bench dips. Maeo 2023 numbers taken from the
sci-sport summary (whole 19.9/13.9%, long 28.5/19.6%) because PubMed blocked the fetch — verify
against the paper before printing. The "pushdown = lateral head" lore marked as contradicted by
ACE 2011 (long 81 > lateral 67 in rope pushdowns).

Open questions:
1. Maeo 2023 per-head numbers for lateral and medial separately: not in any summary found.
2. Kholinne 2018 (long head force share highest at 0° shoulder) vs Maeo 2023 (long head grows most
   overhead): both can be true (force share ≠ stretch-mediated growth) but the site must not say
   "the long head works hardest overhead". Say "is stretched most overhead".
3. Upright vs leaning dips, skull-crusher arm angle, kickback shoulder angle: no data; all
   **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**, wording "by muscle length" on the site.
4. ACE 2011 subjects were 15 women at 70% 1RM; the close-grip bench's 62% is a low-load artefact.
   Never rank the close-grip bench "worst" on the site.
5. All checker thresholds (flare 20/45°, elbow distance 25/40 cm, arm-angle ranges 10/30°, etc.)
   are **[OUR THRESHOLD]**; tune on real captures.
6. Villalba 2024 (supinated handle → more long head in pushdowns) is abstract-only; optional
   "palms-up pushdown" variant, not a core claim.
