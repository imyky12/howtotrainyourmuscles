# Shoulder exercise knowledge base — "How to train your muscles" v1

Research date: 25 Oct 2026 (researcher: Fable). Same conventions as `chest-research.md` and
`back.md`. **[DISAGREEMENT]** = sources split; **[COACHING CONSENSUS]** = coaching practice, no
measured data; **[DERIVED]** = computed by us from a source's geometry.

Conventions:
- **Shoulder abduction** = upper arm away from the side in the frontal plane; 0° = arm at the side,
  90° = arm level with the shoulder, 180° = straight overhead. **Scapular plane** = 30° in front of the
  frontal plane (the natural plane of the shoulder blade).
- **Shoulder flexion** = arm raised forward; **horizontal abduction** = arm swept backwards at
  shoulder height.
- Tempo = eccentric-pause-concentric-pause. Default animation **2-0-1-0**; raises use 2-1-1-0.
- Legend: **Prime mover** (coral), **Helper** (amber), **Stabiliser** (dim).
- Beginner default: 3 x 8-12 (presses) or 3 x 12-15 (raises), 2-3 reps in reserve.

## 1. Shoulder anatomy

| Part | Everyday name | What it does | Which direction biases it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Anterior deltoid** (lateral third of clavicle → deltoid tuberosity) | "Front delt" | Raises the arm **forward** (flexion), helps press overhead, horizontal adduction, internal rotation | Overhead press (Campos 2020: 33% MVIC, highest of 4 exercises; ACE 2014: dumbbell shoulder press best of 10), front raise (Coratella 2020: highest anterior-delt activity among raises), incline/flat pressing (chest file) | Campos 2020 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7706677); ACE 2014 (https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/prosource/september-2014/4972/dynamite-delts-ace-research-identifies-top-shoulder-exercises/); Coratella 2020 (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7503819/) |
| **Lateral (middle) deltoid** (acromion → deltoid tuberosity) | "Side delt" / the "cap" | Raises the arm **sideways** (abduction) | Lateral raise, arm neutral/thumb forward (Campos 2020: 30% MVIC; Coratella 2020: neutral-rotation raise > other raise variants; ACE 2014: bent-arm lateral raise and 45° incline row top). Overhead press is second (28%, Campos 2020) | as above |
| **Posterior deltoid** (spine of scapula → deltoid tuberosity) | "Rear delt" | Pulls the arm **backwards** at shoulder height (horizontal abduction), extension, external rotation | Reverse fly / reverse pec deck (ACE 2014: seated rear lateral raise and 45° incline row top; Schoenfeld 2013: neutral grip > pronated), face pull, rows. Lateral raise also loads it (24% MVIC, Campos 2020) | Schoenfeld 2013 JSCR 27(10):2644 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23302754/) |
| **Rotator cuff** (supraspinatus, infraspinatus, teres minor, subscapularis; scapula → humeral tubercles) | "The shoulder's seat belt" | Pulls the ball of the humerus into the socket in every arm movement; supraspinatus starts abduction, infraspinatus/teres minor rotate the arm outward, subscapularis inward | Not a "look" muscle: light it as **stabiliser** in every press and raise, and as **helper** in the face pull (external rotation at the end) and reverse pec deck (Schoenfeld 2013: infraspinatus higher with a neutral grip). Direct face-pull EMG does not exist **[gap]**; the closest data (band pull-apart, diagonal-up, palm-up: highest infraspinatus, lower trap and posterior delt, 15-73% MVC, IJSPT 2022) supports the pattern | StatPearls rotator cuff (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK513324/); IJSPT 2022 band pull-apart (https://doaj.org/article/878a2765e0b44e53a99acd2dfcf129f3) |

Helpers that must be lit: **upper trapezius** (rotates the scapula upward in every raise and press;
takes over when the shoulders shrug), **serratus anterior** (upward rotation above ~120°, Ekstrom
2003), **triceps** (elbow extension in presses; Saeterbakken 2013: standing barbell press = highest
triceps), **mid/lower trapezius and rhomboids** (retract in the reverse pec deck and face pull),
**pec major clavicular head** (front raise, Coratella 2020), **core/glutes** (standing press: posterior
delt and core work 24-25% higher standing than seated, Saeterbakken 2013), **biceps** (fixed-elbow
raises; barbell press more than dumbbell).

Practical rule for the site: **front** = arm forward/overhead; **side** = arm sideways; **rear** = arm
backwards. Every press works all three (Campos 2020: 33/28/11% MVIC front/side/rear); raises
isolate one direction. Standing beats seated and dumbbells beat barbells for **activation**, barbells
beat dumbbells for **load** (Saeterbakken 2013). No single exercise covers the whole deltoid (ACE
2014).

## 3. The exercises (7)

### 3.1 `bb_ohp` — Standing barbell overhead press
- **Station:** power rack, bar in the J-hooks at upper-chest height.
- **Muscles:** Prime: anterior deltoid (33% MVIC, Campos 2020 — highest of their 4 exercises;
  Saeterbakken 2013: barbell gives ~15% less anterior delt than dumbbells but ~7% more load), lateral
  deltoid (28% MVIC). Helper: triceps (Saeterbakken 2013: highest triceps of the four press
  variants), upper trapezius and serratus anterior (upward rotation of the scapula at the top),
  posterior deltoid (11% MVIC; 25% higher standing than seated). Stabiliser: rotator cuff, core,
  glutes.
- **Real-world flow:** Set the J-hooks so the bar sits at the top of the chest. Load plates. Step under
  the bar, grip just outside the shoulders, elbows under and slightly in front of the bar, bar on the
  upper chest/front delts. Stand up to unrack, take one step back with each foot, feet hip width.
  Do the set. Walk forward until the bar is over the hooks, bend the knees slightly and set it down
  into the J-hooks.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Standing, feet 1x biacromial, knees straight (not locked), torso 0° (ribs down, glutes squeezed;
    lumbar extension ≤10°). Grip ~1.2-1.3x biacromial so the forearms are **vertical** at the start.
  - Start: bar on the upper chest, shoulder flexion ~45° (upper arm pointing down-forward), elbow
    ~60-70°, elbows 2-5 cm in front of the bar, wrists stacked over the elbows.
  - Press: head tilts back ~10° so the bar passes 2-3 cm from the face; bar rises in a **straight
    vertical line over the midfoot**; once the bar passes the forehead the head comes forward
    ("through") so the bar ends **over the mid-foot and the shoulder joint**, elbows 180°, shoulder
    flexion 180°, scapulae upwardly rotated and elevated at lockout (shrug at the top is correct).
  - Lower 2 s along the same line to the upper chest. Tempo 2-0-1-0 (brace on each rep).
- **Steps:** 1 "Bar on upper chest" · 2 "Press straight up" · 3 "Head through, lock out" · 4
  "Lower to chest".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Leaning back (lumbar hyperextension)** — torso goes from 0° to 20-30° backward lean with the
     ribs flared. Metric: **lumbar extension angle** (or torso lean from vertical), good ≤10°, bad ≥20°.
     Now: it becomes an incline press; the lower back takes the load. Later: a sore lower back after
     press days. Cue: "Squeeze glutes, ribs down."
  2. **Bar path arcing forward** — the bar travels 10-15 cm in front of the midfoot at the forehead and
     finishes in front of the shoulders. Metric: **horizontal bar-to-midfoot distance at lockout**, good
     ≤3 cm, bad ≥10 cm. Now: the shoulder works at a longer lever, less weight goes up. Later: elbow
     and shoulder strain from the forward lean. Cue: "Head through, bar over the ears."
  3. **Elbows behind the bar / wrists bent back** — elbow is 5-10 cm behind the bar at the start,
     wrist extension ≥45°. Metric: **wrist extension angle**, good ≤20°, bad ≥45°. Now: the press
     starts from a weak position. Later: wrist pain. Cue: "Elbows under, knuckles up."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 6-10 with the empty bar (20 kg) or a 10-15 kg technique bar. Safety: clear the
  face; do not drop the head forward under the bar; never lean back to grind a rep.

### 3.2 `db_shoulder_press` — Seated dumbbell shoulder press
- **Station:** dumbbell rack + upright-seat bench (back pad 80-90°).
- **Muscles:** Prime: anterior deltoid (ACE 2014: dumbbell shoulder press = best anterior-delt
  exercise of 10; Saeterbakken 2013: seated DB +11% anterior delt vs seated barbell), lateral deltoid.
  Helper: triceps, upper trapezius, serratus. Stabiliser: rotator cuff (more than with a bar), core
  (less than standing: posterior delt and biceps lower seated). Seated barbell was 10% stronger than
  seated dumbbell in 1RM (Saeterbakken 2013).
- **Real-world flow:** Take a pair of dumbbells from the rack. Walk to the upright bench, sit with the
  dumbbells upright on the thighs. Kick the knees up one at a time to bring each dumbbell to the
  shoulder, back against the pad, feet flat. Do the set. Lower the dumbbells to the shoulders, bring
  the knees up to meet them, set them on the thighs, then stand and return them to the rack.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Seated, back pad 80-90°, hips and upper back on the pad, feet flat, knees ~90°.
  - Start: dumbbells at shoulder/ear height, palms forward (or 45° in), shoulder abduction ~80-90°,
    elbow ~90°, upper arm in the scapular plane (elbows 20-30° in front of the shoulder line, not
    pinned back to a "T").
  - Press: dumbbells rise and converge to finish ~10 cm apart over the shoulders, elbow 180°,
    shoulder abduction ~170-180°, scapulae upwardly rotate.
  - Lower 2 s to ear height (elbow ~90°; dumbbells level with the ears, upper arm slightly below
    shoulder height). Tempo 2-0-1-0.
- **Steps:** 1 "Dumbbells at ear height" · 2 "Press up and in" · 3 "Lock out over shoulders" · 4
  "Lower to ears".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Half reps** — dumbbells stop at the forehead; elbow stays at ≥120°. Metric: **elbow angle at the
     bottom**, good ≤95°, bad ≥120°. Now: the stretched range is skipped. Later: less growth for the
     same sets. Cue: "Dumbbells to the ears."
  2. **Elbows flared to a "T", too deep** — upper arm in the frontal plane and dumbbells below the
     shoulder (elbow 60°). Metric: **upper-arm angle in front of the frontal plane** (0° = T), good
     20-30°, bad ≤5°. Now: more front-shoulder strain, less press. Later: front shoulder irritation
     under heavy loads **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**. Cue: "Elbows slightly forward."
  3. **Arching off the pad** — lower back leaves the pad, ribs flare, pelvis tilts 15-20°. Metric:
     **lumbar-to-pad gap**, good ≤3 cm, bad ≥8 cm. Now: it becomes an incline press. Later: sore lower
     back. Cue: "Ribs down, back on the pad."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 8-12 with dumbbells you can kick up alone. Safety: never drop the dumbbells; get
  them to the thighs first. A spotter can hand them up for heavy sets.

### 3.3 `db_lateral_raise` — Dumbbell lateral raise
- **Station:** dumbbell rack (standing beside it).
- **Muscles:** Prime: lateral deltoid (Campos 2020: 30% MVIC, highest of their 4 exercises; ACE 2014:
  bent-arm lateral raise top for medial delt; Coratella 2020: neutral rotation = most medial delt).
  Helper: supraspinatus (starts the raise), anterior and posterior deltoid (21%/24% MVIC, Campos
  2020), upper trapezius and serratus (scapular upward rotation). Stabiliser: core. Dumbbell
  resistance peaks at the top (Larsen 2025) — the squeeze at shoulder height matters.
- **Real-world flow:** Take a pair of light dumbbells from the rack, step one pace away from the rack,
  stand feet hip width, dumbbells at the sides. Do the set. Return the dumbbells to the rack.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Standing, torso 0-10° forward lean (fixed), knees soft, arms at the sides, elbows bent 10-20°
    and **fixed**, palms facing the thighs.
  - Raise: both arms rise in the **scapular plane** (hands ~30° in front of the frontal plane) to
    shoulder abduction ~80-90°: **elbows level with the shoulders, hands level with or just below
    the elbows**, thumb level or slightly up (neutral/"full can"), not thumbs-down. Shoulders stay
    down (acromion rises ≤2 cm). ~85° of travel.
  - Pause 1 s, lower 2 s to ~15° abduction (not all the way to touching the legs: keeps tension).
    Tempo 2-1-1-1. Dumbbell path: a wide arc; the elbow leads, not the hand.
- **Steps:** 1 "Stand tall, soft elbows" · 2 "Raise to shoulder height" · 3 "Hold one second" · 4
  "Lower slowly".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Swinging with the torso** — torso rocks from 10° forward to 10° back on every rep; dumbbells
     fly above the shoulders. Metric: **torso lean change within the rep**, good ≤5°, bad ≥15°. Now:
     momentum, not the side delt, lifts the weight. Later: more weight on the rack, same shoulders.
     Cue: "Lighter weight, body still."
  2. **Shrugging** — shoulders rise 4-6 cm toward the ears, upper traps bulge. Metric: **acromion
     height change during the raise**, good ≤2 cm, bad ≥4 cm. Now: upper traps steal the work. Later:
     tight traps, flat shoulders. Cue: "Shoulders down, reach wide."
  3. **Hands above the elbows / thumbs down ("pouring")** — wrist 5-10 cm higher than the elbow, upper
     arm internally rotated. Metric: **hand height minus elbow height at the top**, good ≤0 cm, bad
     ≥5 cm. Now: shifts the load to the rear delt and traps (Coratella 2020). Later: thumb-down
     end-range raises are the position most shoulder-impingement advice warns against
     **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**. Cue: "Elbows lead, thumbs level."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 12-15, 2-5 kg. Safety: light weight, no swing; stop at shoulder height.

### 3.4 `cable_lateral_raise` — Cable lateral raise
- **Station:** dual cable tower, pulley at the lowest setting (or ~hip height for the stretched
  version), D-handle.
- **Muscles:** Same as 3.3: Prime lateral deltoid; helper supraspinatus, anterior/posterior delt,
  upper trap. The cable keeps tension at the bottom where the dumbbell has none (Larsen 2025); growth
  over 8 weeks was the same as with dumbbells (+3.3-4.6% thickness), so this is the "constant
  tension" variant, not a "better" one — say so on screen.
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pulley to the bottom, clip the D-handle, set a light pin. Stand side-on
  to the tower, grab the handle with the far hand so the cable crosses in front of the body, step
  half a pace away so the stack lifts, hold the tower with the near hand. Do the set. Lower the handle
  to the start, step in, release, turn around and repeat with the other arm. Unclip the handle at the
  end.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Standing side-on, feet hip width, near hand on the tower, torso 0-10° lean **away** from the
    tower is acceptable but fixed. Elbow 10-20° bent and fixed.
  - Start: working arm across the front of the body, hand at the opposite hip (shoulder abduction
    ~-10°; this is the extra stretch the cable gives).
  - Raise: arm sweeps out in the scapular plane to abduction ~85-90°, elbow at shoulder height, hand
    level with the elbow, thumb level. Shoulders down.
  - Pause 1 s, lower 2-3 s back across the body. Tempo 3-1-1-1.
- **Steps:** 1 "Handle at far hip" · 2 "Sweep out to shoulder height" · 3 "Hold one second" · 4
  "Lower across the body".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Leaning into the tower** — torso tilts 20-30° toward the tower, hips swing out. Metric:
     **torso lateral lean angle**, good ≤10°, bad ≥20°. Now: the body does the lifting. Later: no
     extra delt, strain on the lower back. Cue: "Hips still, only the arm moves."
  2. **Bending the elbow (becomes a curl-raise)** — elbow 165° → 110°. Metric: **elbow angle change
     within the rep**, good ≤10°, bad ≥30°. Now: shorter lever = less delt work. Later: biceps grow,
     delts do not. Cue: "Elbow angle never changes."
  3. **Shrugging** — same metric as 3.3 (acromion rise, good ≤2 cm, bad ≥4 cm). Cue: "Shoulder down,
     reach wide."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 12-15 each arm, lightest plates. Safety: let the stack settle before releasing.

### 3.5 `db_front_raise` — Dumbbell front raise
- **Station:** dumbbell rack.
- **Muscles:** Prime: anterior deltoid (Coratella 2020: highest anterior-delt activity of the raise
  variants). Helper: pec major clavicular head (Coratella 2020: more pec than any lateral raise),
  lateral deltoid, upper trapezius and serratus (upward rotation). Stabiliser: core, biceps (holds the
  elbow). Tested but unranked in ACE 2014. Note for the plan: the front delt already gets the most
  work from presses (Campos 2020: 33% MVIC), so this is an optional finisher.
- **Real-world flow:** Take a pair of light dumbbells from the rack, step back, stand feet hip width,
  dumbbells resting against the front of the thighs. Do the set (both arms together, or alternating).
  Return the dumbbells to the rack.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Standing, torso 0-5°, knees soft, elbows bent 10-15° and fixed, palms facing the thighs or down.
  - Raise: shoulder flexion 0° → ~90° (dumbbells level with the shoulder, straight in front, hands
    1x biacromial apart). Shoulders stay down. Not above eye level.
  - Pause 1 s, lower 2 s to the thighs. Tempo 2-1-1-0.
- **Steps:** 1 "Dumbbells on thighs" · 2 "Raise to shoulder height" · 3 "Hold one second" · 4
  "Lower slowly".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Swinging / leaning back** — torso goes to 15° backward lean on each raise. Metric: **torso lean
     change within the rep**, good ≤5°, bad ≥15°. Now: momentum lifts the weight. Later: lower back
     takes the strain. Cue: "Body still, arms only."
  2. **Raising above the head** — flexion 130°+; traps take over above ~90°. Metric: **shoulder
     flexion at the top**, good 85-95°, bad ≥120°. Now: traps and serratus do the top part. Later:
     wasted range, nothing dangerous. Cue: "Stop at shoulder height."
  3. **Shrugging** — acromion rises ≥4 cm (same metric as 3.3). Cue: "Shoulders down."
- **Beginner:** 2 x 12-15, 2-5 kg. Safety: light; skip it if the front of the shoulder is sore after
  pressing.

### 3.6 `reverse_pec_deck` — Reverse pec deck (rear-delt fly)
- **Station:** pec deck with the arms swung to the rear-delt position; sit facing the pad.
- **Muscles:** Prime: posterior deltoid (ACE 2014: the seated rear lateral raise, the free-weight
  version of this movement, was top for the rear delt; Schoenfeld 2013: neutral grip adds a small
  amount of posterior delt and infraspinatus over a pronated grip). Helper: mid trapezius and
  rhomboids (retraction at the end; Ekstrom 2003: horizontal extension with external rotation =
  highest mid-trap exercise), infraspinatus/teres minor, lateral deltoid. Stabiliser: erector spinae
  (upright).
- **Real-world flow:** Set the handles to the rear-delt position (fully back) and the seat so the
  handles are at shoulder height. Set the pin. Sit facing the pad, chest against it, feet flat. Reach
  forward and grip the handles with a **neutral grip** (palms facing each other), arms straight in
  front, elbows slightly bent. Do the set. Bring the handles back together under control, release,
  stand up.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Seated, chest on the pad, torso 0-5°, feet flat. Handles at shoulder height. Elbow 160-170°
    (10-20° bend), fixed.
  - Start: arms straight forward, hands together, shoulder flexion ~90° (horizontal abduction 0°),
    scapulae protracted.
  - Pull: arms sweep out and back in the horizontal plane to **horizontal abduction ~20-30° behind
    the shoulder line** (elbows just behind the torso), hands at shoulder height; scapulae retract
    in the last third only. Shoulders down.
  - Pause 1 s, return 2 s to hands-together without letting the stack rest. Tempo 2-1-1-1.
- **Steps:** 1 "Chest on pad, neutral grip" · 2 "Sweep arms back" · 3 "Hold one second" · 4
  "Return slowly".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Shrugging and arm-bending (turns into a row)** — elbow goes 165° → 90°, shoulders rise 4-6 cm.
     Metric: **elbow angle change within the rep**, good ≤10°, bad ≥40°. Now: traps and rhomboids do
     a row, the rear delt is bypassed. Later: little rear-delt growth. Cue: "Long arms, sweep, don't
     row."
  2. **Hands too high / too low** — hands finish 10-15 cm above or below the shoulders. Metric: **hand
     height minus shoulder height at the end**, good ±5 cm, bad ≥10 cm either way. Now: above = upper
     traps; below = lats. Later: rear delt undertrained. Cue: "Hands level with the shoulders."
  3. **Letting the stack drop** — the stack touches down each rep and the arms rest at the front.
     Metric: **minimum cable tension / stack height**, good >0 (stack stays off), bad = 0. Now: tension
     lost on every rep. Later: fewer effective reps. Cue: "Stop just before the plates touch."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 12-15, light. Safety: no jerking the handles back; the rear delt is a small muscle.

### 3.7 `face_pull` — Cable face pull
- **Station:** dual cable tower, pulley at forehead height (or slightly above), rope attachment.
- **Muscles:** Prime: posterior deltoid, infraspinatus/teres minor (external rotation at the end),
  mid and lower trapezius, rhomboids (retraction). Helper: lateral deltoid, biceps. Stabiliser: core,
  erector spinae. Evidence: no direct face-pull EMG **[gap]**; the pattern (horizontal abduction +
  external rotation) is the one Ekstrom 2003 found best for the mid trap and the IJSPT 2022 band
  pull-apart (diagonal-up, palm-up) found best for infraspinatus, lower trap and posterior delt. NASM
  lists posterior delt as the prime mover. Word it on the site as "rear delt and rotator cuff" without
  percentages.
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pulley to forehead height, clip the rope, set a light pin. Grip the rope
  ends with the thumbs toward you (palms in, overhand-neutral), step back two paces until the arms
  are straight and the stack is up, feet staggered or hip width. Do the set. Walk forward to let the
  stack down, release the rope.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Standing, feet hip width (or one foot back), torso 0-10° back lean, fixed. Rope at forehead
    height, arms straight forward (shoulder flexion ~100-110°, slightly above the shoulder line).
  - Pull: elbows travel out and back **at shoulder height** (elbow flare ~90° from the trunk,
    horizontal abduction to ~20° behind the shoulder line), elbows end ~90° flexed; hands split past
    the ears so the **rope ends finish beside the ears / temples**, forearms rotating up (external
    rotation: hand above the elbow, ~45-60° above horizontal at the end). Scapulae retract and
    depress. Shoulders down.
  - Pause 1 s, return 2 s to straight arms. Tempo 2-1-1-1. Hand path: a widening "V" toward the
    face.
- **Steps:** 1 "Rope at forehead height" · 2 "Pull to the ears" · 3 "Elbows high, rotate up" · 4
  "Return slowly".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Elbows dropping (becomes a row)** — elbows end at hip/rib height, 20-30 cm below the shoulders.
     Metric: **elbow height minus shoulder height at the end**, good ≥-5 cm, bad ≤-20 cm. Now: lats
     and mid traps row the weight; rear delts and cuff are bypassed. Later: the rear delt stays
     small. Cue: "Elbows high, pull to the face."
  2. **Leaning back / swinging** — torso goes to 20-30° back lean, hips push forward. Metric: **torso
     lean change within the rep**, good ≤5°, bad ≥15°. Now: body weight pulls the stack. Later: lower
     back strain with heavier pins. Cue: "Lighter pin, body still."
  3. **No external rotation (hands stay level with elbows)** — forearm ends horizontal, hands at chin
     height in front of the face. Metric: **forearm angle above horizontal at the end**, good ≥45°,
     bad ≤10°. Now: rotator cuff part of the exercise is skipped. Later: less cuff work for the same
     sets. Cue: "Show your biceps at the end."
- **Beginner:** 2-3 x 15-20, light. Safety: this is a light, high-rep exercise; never a heavy one.

## 4. Today's plan — Shoulders (13 hard sets)

| # | Exercise | Sets x reps | Rest | Why it is in the plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Standing barbell overhead press | 3 x 6-8 | 2-3 min | **Front delt** (33% MVIC, Campos 2020) with the most load; side delt second; compound first |
| 2 | Dumbbell lateral raise | 3 x 12-15 | 60-90 s | **Side delt** isolation (30% MVIC, Campos 2020; ACE 2014 top medial) |
| 3 | Reverse pec deck | 3 x 12-15 | 60-90 s | **Rear delt** isolation (ACE 2014 top posterior; neutral grip, Schoenfeld 2013) |
| 4 | Cable face pull | 2 x 15-20 | 60 s | **Rear delt + rotator cuff + mid/lower traps** (Ekstrom 2003 pattern) |
| 5 | Dumbbell front raise (optional) | 2 x 12-15 | 60 s | **Front delt** finisher; skip when pressing volume is already high |

Total 13 sets (11 without the front raise). Parts covered: front delt (1, 5), side delt (1, 2), rear
delt (3, 4), rotator cuff (4 as helper; stabiliser in 1, 2). The deltoid also gets front-delt work on
chest day and rear-delt work on back day, so 11-13 direct sets here is enough for the 10-20
sets/week range.

**Alternative plan — no barbell (dumbbells, cables, machines only), 11 sets:**
seated dumbbell shoulder press 3 x 8-12 (front delt; ACE 2014 best anterior) · cable lateral raise
3 x 12-15 each arm (side delt, constant tension) · reverse pec deck 3 x 12-15 (rear delt) · cable face
pull 2 x 15-20 (rear delt, rotator cuff).

## 6. Changelog and open questions

**Changes to the proposed list:** none. All seven exercises kept. Notes:
- Front raise kept but marked **optional** in the plan: the front delt is already the most-worked
  head in every press (Campos 2020: 33% MVIC in the shoulder press), and Coratella 2020 shows the
  front raise also loads the pec. If the owner wants a tighter plan, drop it to 11 sets.
- Reverse pec deck is specified with a **neutral grip** (Schoenfeld 2013; difference small, CI 0.1-7.4%
  MVIC).
- Cable lateral raise is presented as an equal alternative to the dumbbell raise, not an upgrade
  (Larsen 2025: same hypertrophy).

**Open questions:**
1. No EMG study of the cable face pull exists; the muscle list is from the movement pattern (Ekstrom
   2003, IJSPT 2022 band pull-apart) and NASM. The site should not print face-pull percentages.
2. Saeterbakken 2013 1RM percentages (7% / 10%) come from Breaking Muscle's write-up; the EMG
   percentages are consistent across two secondary sources. Recheck against the paper before putting
   them on screen.
3. ACE 2014 %MVC values are only in the article's figures (not read); the rankings are quoted, not
   the numbers.
4. Lateral raise elbow bend (10-20°), scapular-plane angle (30°) and "hands not above elbows" are
   coaching conventions; the supraspinatus/"full can" literature supports the direction but no study
   tested the raise itself at those angles.
5. Seated dumbbell press back-pad angle: 80-90° is convention; no EMG by pad angle found.
6. Overhead press joint angles at the start (elbow 60-70°, shoulder flexion 45°) are [DERIVED] from
   "bar on the upper chest, forearms vertical, grip just outside the shoulders"; the 3D model should
   derive them from limb lengths and report them.
