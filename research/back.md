# Back exercise knowledge base — "How to train your muscles" v1

Research date: 25 Oct 2026 (researcher: Fable). Same conventions as `chest-research.md`: every number
that reaches the animation or on-screen text traces to a source below. **[DISAGREEMENT]** = sources
split; **[COACHING CONSENSUS]** = coaching practice, no measured data; **[DERIVED]** = a number we
computed from a source's geometry, not one the source printed.

Conventions (shared with chest):
- Grip/stance width as a multiple of **biacromial width** (shoulder tip to tip). 1.5x is the research
  default for pull-ups and pulldowns (Snarr 2017, Padovan 2024).
- **Torso hinge angle** = angle of the trunk (hip-to-shoulder line) from vertical. 0° = upright,
  90° = parallel to the floor.
- **Shoulder extension** = upper arm travelling backwards past the trunk line (negative flexion).
- Tempo = eccentric-pause-concentric-pause in seconds. Default animation **2-0-1-0**; studies used
  2-0.5-2 (Padovan 2024/2025). Schoenfeld 2015: 0.5-8 s per rep grows muscle equally.
- Highlight legend: **Prime mover** (coral), **Helper** (amber), **Stabiliser** (dim).
- Beginner default: 3 x 8-12, 2-3 reps in reserve, add weight when every set hits 12 clean reps.

## 1. Back anatomy (what the site needs, no more)

| Part | Everyday name | What it does | Which direction biases it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Latissimus dorsi** (origin: T7-sacrum via thoracolumbar fascia, iliac crest, lower ribs; insertion: floor of the bicipital groove of the humerus) | "Lats" / the "wings" | Pulls the upper arm **down and back** toward the body (shoulder extension, adduction, internal rotation) | Any pull where the elbow travels from overhead or in front **down/back to the ribs**: pull-up, pulldown, straight-arm pulldown, rows (elbow close to the body). Highest measured values: pull-up 117-130% MVIC (Youdas 2010); ACE 2018: pull-up and chin-up beat six other exercises | Youdas 2010 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21068680/ via Breaking Muscle summary); ACE/Edelburg & Porcari 2018 (https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/april-2018/6959/ace-sponsored-research-what-is-the-best-back-exercise/) |
| **Trapezius, upper fibres** (occiput/C-spine → lateral clavicle, acromion) | "Upper traps" / the "neck slope" | Lifts the shoulder girdle (scapular elevation) and helps rotate the scapula upwards | Straight **vertical shrug**; also isometric in deadlift lockout. Ekstrom 2003: unilateral shrug = highest upper-trap EMG of 10 exercises | Ekstrom, Donatelli & Soderberg 2003, JOSPT 33(5):247 (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12774999/) |
| **Trapezius, middle fibres** (T1-T5 → acromion/spine of scapula) | "Mid traps" / "between the shoulder blades" | Pulls the shoulder blades **together** (retraction) | **Horizontal rows** with the elbows out (seated row, bent-over row), wide-grip rows. Lehman 2004: seated row = highest mid-trap/rhomboid EMG; Padovan 2025: wide grip > narrow for all three trap parts | Lehman 2004, Dyn Med 3:4 (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC449729); Padovan 2025 JHK (https://johk.pl/?p=9499) |
| **Trapezius, lower fibres** (T6-T12 → spine of scapula) | "Lower traps" | Pulls the shoulder blades **down** (depression) and rotates them upward; keeps shoulders "away from the ears" overhead | **Vertical pulls** from overhead (pull-up more than chin-up, Youdas 2010: 45-56% MVIC) and Y-raises. ACE 2018: I-Y-T raise best, bent-over row second | Youdas 2010; ACE 2018 |
| **Rhomboids** (major/minor; C7-T5 → medial scapular border) | "Shoulder-blade squeezers" | Retract and downwardly rotate the scapula; lie **under** the mid traps | Same as mid traps: horizontal rows, scapular squeeze at the end of a row. **No surface-EMG data of their own** (deep muscle; studies record "mid trap/rhomboid" together, Lehman 2004) | Lehman 2004 |
| **Teres major** (inferior angle of scapula → medial lip of bicipital groove, next to the lat) | "The lat's little helper" | Same actions as the lat: extension, adduction, internal rotation of the arm | Every lat movement, strongest when the arm comes **down from overhead** (pulldown, straight-arm pulldown). No exercise-specific EMG found **[gap]**; light it with the lats as a helper | Kenhub anatomy (https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/teres-major-muscle) |
| **Erector spinae** (iliocostalis, longissimus, spinalis; sacrum/iliac crest → ribs, vertebrae, skull) | "Lower back" / "spinal muscles" | Straightens the spine (extension) and holds it rigid against a load that wants to bend it forward | **Hinge** movements: deadlift (prime mover of the hip-hinge lockout with glutes/hamstrings), bent-over row (ACE 2018: highest erector EMG of 8 back exercises; Fenwick 2009: largest lumbar load) | ACE 2018; Fenwick, Brown & McGill 2009, JSCR 23(5):1408 (https://www.backfitpro.com/medical-scientific-articles/2009/[80]Fenwick,C.M.J.(2009)Comparison-of-different-rowing-exercises[J.Strength-and-Cond.].pdf) |

Helpers that must be lit: **biceps/brachialis/brachioradialis** (every bent-arm pull; chin-up and
supinated grips raise biceps, Youdas 2010 78-96% MVIC), **posterior deltoid** (rows, pull-ups: Snarr
2017 reports ~100% MVC in pull-ups), **infraspinatus/teres minor** (71-79% MVIC in pull-ups,
Youdas 2010), **glutes + hamstrings** (prime movers of the deadlift hip extension, Escamilla 2002),
**forearm flexors** (grip in every exercise), **core/obliques** (31-35% MVIC pull-ups; anti-rotation in
one-arm row, Fenwick 2009).

Practical rule for the site (supported by the EMG above): **vertical pulls** (arm comes down from
overhead) = lats, teres major, lower traps; **horizontal pulls** (arm comes back toward the ribs) = mid
traps, rhomboids, rear delts, with lats still working; **hinge** = erector spinae, glutes, hamstrings;
**shrug** = upper traps. Grip width changes little for the lats (Lusk 2010, Andersen 2014); grip
**orientation** does (pronated > supinated for lats, Lusk 2010).

## 3. The exercises (8)

### 3.1 `pullup` — Pull-up (pronated grip)
- **Station:** pull-up/dip station.
- **Muscles:** Prime: lats (117-130% MVIC), teres major. Helper: biceps/brachialis (78-96%), lower
  trap (45-56%), infraspinatus (71-79%), posterior deltoid, pec major (44-57%). Stabiliser: core
  (external oblique 31-35%), erector spinae (39-41%). Evidence: Youdas 2010; Snarr 2017; ACE 2018
  (best lat exercise of 8). Pronated grip = more lower trap, less biceps than a chin-up.
- **Real-world flow:** Walk under the bar. Reach up and grip it palms away, hands 1.5x shoulder
  width (step on the foot rest or jump if the bar is high). Let the body hang straight, legs together
  (or crossed at the ankles, knees bent ~45°). Do the set. Lower to a full hang, then let go and land
  softly, knees bent. Beginners: band or assisted machine not in this gym, so use a jump-up and a
  slow 3-5 s lower (negative).
- **Animation spec:**
  - Start (dead hang): shoulder flexion 180° (arms straight overhead), elbow 180°, scapulae elevated
    and upwardly rotated. Body vertical, hip 180°, knees 180° or crossed/bent 45°.
  - Phase 1 (initiate): scapulae depress and retract ~2-3 cm *before* the elbows bend.
  - Phase 2 (pull): elbows bend to ~90° (Youdas: 93° total elbow ROM), elbows travel down and slightly
    back, chin passes the bar; shoulder flexion ends ~30-45° (upper arm ~45° from the trunk, pointing
    down-and-forward). Chest ~5 cm below the bar. Trunk may lean back ≤15°.
  - Phase 3 (lower): same path reversed over 2 s to a full hang with straight arms; scapulae let
    rise at the bottom ("stretch").
  - Tempo 2-0-1-1 (pause at the top). Grip 1.5x biacromial, pronated. Hand path: vertical.
- **Steps:** 1 "Hang, shoulders down" · 2 "Pull chin over bar" · 3 "Lower slowly" · 4 "Full stretch".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Kipping / swinging** — hips swing forward then back; hip angle changes 180° → ~140° and the
     feet travel 20-40 cm forward. Metric: **hip angle change during the rep**, good ≤10°, bad ≥30°.
     Now: momentum does the work, lats get less. Later: you plateau because the muscle never gets the
     full load. Cue: "Legs still, pull with the back."
  2. **Half reps at the top** — chin stays 5-10 cm below the bar; elbow never passes 110°. Metric:
     **chin height relative to the bar at the top**, good ≥0 cm (above), bad ≤-5 cm. Now: the
     shortened half of the lat and the lower traps are skipped. Later: slower strength gains at the
     top of the movement. Cue: "Chin over the bar every rep."
  3. **No full hang** — elbow stays at ~140° at the bottom. Metric: **elbow angle at the bottom**,
     good ≥170°, bad ≤150°. Now: you cut the stretched range, which is the range that grows muscle
     most (Wolf 2023). Later: less growth per rep. Cue: "Straight arms at the bottom."
- **Beginner:** 3 sets of as many clean reps as possible (minus 2), or 3 x 5 slow negatives (jump up,
  lower in 3-5 s). Safety: no swinging at the bottom; land with bent knees.

### 3.2 `lat_pulldown` — Lat pulldown
- **Station:** lat pulldown machine, wide straight bar.
- **Muscles:** Prime: lats, teres major. Helper: biceps, lower trap, posterior deltoid, infraspinatus,
  pec major (front version). Evidence: Lusk 2010 (pronated > supinated for lats; width irrelevant);
  Andersen 2014 (1-1.5x biacromial strongest); Sperandei 2009 and Padovan 2024 (pull to the front,
  not behind the neck: same or more lat, 8RM 70 vs 59 kg, less shoulder strain). Lower-trap and lat
  EMG are below the pull-up (ACE 2018) because the load is lower, not because the pattern is worse.
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pin. Adjust the thigh pad so it rests on the thighs when seated, feet
  flat. Stand up, reach and grip the bar palms away at ~1.5x shoulder width, then sit down pulling
  the bar with you and slide the thighs under the pad. Arms straight overhead, lean back slightly.
  Do the set. Stand up with the bar under control and let it return to the stack, release.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Seated, thighs locked, feet flat, knees ~90°. Torso lean back 10-20° from vertical, held for the
    whole set (**[DISAGREEMENT]**: NASM says 20-30°, coaching sites 10-20°; both agree it must not
    change during the rep). Grip 1.5x biacromial, pronated.
  - Start: shoulder flexion ~170° (arms up, slight forward lean of the bar line), elbow 180°,
    scapulae elevated.
  - Pull: scapulae depress first, then elbows drive down and slightly back to end at or just behind
    the ribs; shoulder ends ~30° flexion / upper arm ~45° from the trunk; elbow ~70-90°; **bar
    touches the upper chest at collarbone level** (Padovan 2024: "mid-chest in front of the head").
  - Return 2 s to straight arms, shoulder blades rising at the top.
  - Bar path: nearly vertical, passing ~5 cm in front of the face. Tempo 2-0-1-1.
- **Steps:** 1 "Sit tall, lean back slightly" · 2 "Pull bar to collarbones" · 3 "Squeeze elbows
  down" · 4 "Return to straight arms".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Leaning back into a row** — torso goes from 15° to ≥45° lean during the pull and the bar ends at
     the belly. Metric: **torso lean change within the rep**, good ≤10°, bad ≥25°. Now: it becomes a
     half row with momentum; lats get less vertical pull. Later: you load more than the lats can
     handle and the lower back does the work. Cue: "Lean once, then stay still."
  2. **Behind-the-neck pull** — head pushed forward 10-15 cm, bar ends behind the neck. Metric: **bar
     position relative to the chin at the bottom**, good ≥5 cm in front, bad behind. Now: 15% less
     load lifted (59 vs 70 kg) and no more lat (Padovan 2024). Later: repeated end-range external
     rotation under load irritates the front of the shoulder in some people. Cue: "Bar to the
     collarbones, chin up."
  3. **Half reps / no stretch** — elbow stays at ≤140° at the top. Metric: **elbow angle at the top**,
     good ≥170°, bad ≤150°. Now: you skip the stretched range. Later: less growth from the same
     sets. Cue: "Let the arms go straight."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 8-12. Safety: do not let the stack slam; release the bar only when standing.

### 3.3 `bb_row` — Barbell bent-over row
- **Station:** barbell on the deadlift platform (loaded on the floor) or taken from the rack J-hooks.
- **Muscles:** Prime: lats, mid trap/rhomboids, posterior deltoid. Helper: biceps, lower trap, teres
  major, brachioradialis. Stabiliser (heavy): **erector spinae** (ACE 2018: highest of 8 back
  exercises), glutes/hamstrings (isometric hinge), core. Evidence: ACE 2018 (top for mid trap, second
  for lower trap); Fenwick 2009 (large symmetric back activation, largest lumbar load → the hinge
  must be held rigid).
- **Real-world flow:** Stand behind the bar on the platform, feet hip width, bar over the midfoot. Hinge
  at the hips with a flat back, grip just outside the knees palms down (~1.2-1.5x shoulder width).
  Lift the bar to the start: stand up, then hinge to ~45° torso lean so the bar hangs below the
  chest, knees slightly bent. Do the set. Finish by hinging further and setting the bar down on the
  platform with a flat back, then stand.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Torso hinge 45° from vertical, held fixed (**[COACHING CONSENSUS]**: NSCA coaching range ~45° for
    novices; more horizontal = more lumbar shear, Fenwick/McGill). Knees 15-20° bent, feet
    hip-width, weight midfoot. Neutral spine (no lumbar rounding: lumbar flexion ≤10°).
  - Start: arms hanging straight, shoulder flexion ~45° relative to the trunk (bar below the
    chest), scapulae protracted (stretch).
  - Pull: elbows drive back past the trunk; shoulder extension to ~-20° (elbow behind the back),
    elbow flexion ~90°; elbow flare 30-45° from the trunk; scapulae retract 3-5 cm; **bar touches
    the lower ribs / upper belly**.
  - Lower 2 s to straight arms, scapulae protract. Bar path: slight arc toward the body. Tempo
    2-1-1-0.
- **Steps:** 1 "Hinge, flat back" · 2 "Row bar to ribs" · 3 "Squeeze shoulder blades" · 4 "Lower
  with control".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Torso heaving** — torso swings from 45° to ~20° lean on every pull. Metric: **torso lean
     change within the rep**, good ≤10°, bad ≥20°. Now: the lower back and hips lift the bar, not the
     lats. Later: you keep adding weight the back muscles never actually move. Cue: "Freeze the
     torso, move only the arms."
  2. **Rounded lower back** — lumbar flexion 20-30°, hips tucked under. Metric: **lumbar flexion
     angle**, good ≤10°, bad ≥20°. Now: disc shear rises with flexion plus a forward lean (SBS;
     Cholewicki 1991). Later: a stiff, sore lower back after rowing days. Cue: "Chest up, hips
     back."
  3. **Elbows flared to 90°, bar to the chest** — upper arm level with the shoulder. Metric: **elbow
     flare angle at the top** (humerus vs trunk axis in the coronal plane), good 30-45°, bad ≥75°.
     Now: becomes a rear-delt row; lats contribute less. Later: shoulder front gets irritated under
     heavy loads. Cue: "Elbows to the hips."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 8-10, start with the empty bar (20 kg). Safety: stop the set when the hinge angle
  starts drifting; this exercise loads the lower back more than any other row (Fenwick 2009).

### 3.4 `db_row` — One-arm dumbbell row
- **Station:** dumbbell rack + flat bench next to it.
- **Muscles:** Prime: lats, teres major, posterior deltoid, mid trap/rhomboids. Helper: biceps,
  brachioradialis, lower trap. Stabiliser: obliques/core (anti-rotation; Fenwick 2009 found the
  one-arm cable row mainly challenged trunk rotation control), erector spinae (light: the bench
  supports the torso). Evidence: no direct one-arm-vs-barbell EMG found **[gap]**; ACE 2018 and
  Fenwick 2009 for row patterns in general.
- **Real-world flow:** Take one dumbbell from the rack with the right hand. Walk to the flat bench. Put
  the left knee and left hand on the bench, right foot on the floor beside the bench, torso near
  horizontal, dumbbell hanging straight down. Do the set. Set the dumbbell on the floor, stand up,
  switch sides (right knee and hand on the bench). After both sides, return the dumbbell to the rack.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Torso hinge 70-80° from vertical (close to parallel), spine neutral, support knee on the bench
    at 90°, support arm straight. Shoulders square to the floor (shoulder line rotation ≤10°).
  - Start: working arm hangs straight (elbow 180°), scapula protracted, dumbbell ~10 cm below
    bench height.
  - Pull: elbow drives up and back along the ribs (elbow flare ~30°), elbow ends ~90°, upper arm
    ends ~15-20° behind the trunk line, **dumbbell handle reaches the hip/lower rib**, scapula
    retracts 3-5 cm.
  - Lower 2 s to a full hang with the shoulder blade reaching forward (stretch). Dumbbell path: a
    slight arc from under the shoulder to the hip. Tempo 2-1-1-0.
- **Steps:** 1 "Knee and hand on bench" · 2 "Row to the hip" · 3 "Squeeze at the top" · 4 "Lower
  and stretch".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Torso twist** — the working shoulder rotates up 25-40° to lift the weight. Metric: **shoulder
     line rotation vs the floor**, good ≤10°, bad ≥25°. Now: the twist, not the lat, moves the weight.
     Later: one side gets stronger than the other. Cue: "Shoulders flat to the floor."
  2. **Elbow flared, pulling to the armpit** — elbow travels out at 70-90° and the dumbbell ends under
     the chest. Metric: **elbow flare angle at the top**, good ≤45°, bad ≥70°. Now: rear delt takes
     over from the lat. Later: slower lat development. Cue: "Elbow to the back pocket."
  3. **Short range / no stretch** — elbow stays at ~150° at the bottom. Metric: **elbow angle at the
     bottom**, good ≥170°, bad ≤150°. Now: no stretched-position work. Later: less growth per set.
     Cue: "Let it hang, then row."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-12 each side. Safety: keep the back flat; the bench, not the spine, holds the
  torso.

### 3.5 `cable_row` — Seated cable row
- **Station:** seated cable row machine, close neutral (triangle) handle.
- **Muscles:** Prime: lats (narrow grip emphasis, Padovan 2025b), mid trap/rhomboids (Lehman 2004:
  highest mid-trap/rhomboid activity of the four exercises tested; ACE 2018: among the best for mid
  trap). Helper: posterior deltoid, biceps, lower trap, teres major. Stabiliser: erector spinae
  (isometric), core. Evidence: Lehman 2004; Padovan 2025a/b. Actively "squeezing" the scapula did
  **not** add mid-trap activity (Lehman 2004) and a fixed vs free scapula changed activation by only
  4-7% (Padovan 2025a) — the site should say "let the shoulder blades move naturally", not "pinch
  hard".
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pin. Sit on the pad, place the feet on the footplates, knees slightly
  bent. Lean forward, grab the handle with both hands, then sit back upright so the stack lifts and
  the arms are straight. Do the set. Lean forward with the handle under control until the stack
  rests, release, stand up.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Seated, knees 15-30° bent, torso upright 0-10° from vertical and **fixed** for the rep (Padovan
    2025a: "minimise trunk flexion and extension").
  - Start: arms straight forward (shoulder flexion ~90°), scapulae protracted, handle at knee height.
  - Pull: elbows travel straight back along the ribs (elbow flare ≤20° with the neutral handle),
    elbow ends ~80-90°, upper arm ends ~15° behind the trunk, **handle touches the navel / lower
    belly** (Padovan 2025a: "until the handle reaches the abdominal region"), scapulae retract.
  - Return 2 s to straight arms, scapulae protract. Handle path: horizontal. Tempo 2-1-1-0
    (studies used 2-0.5-2).
- **Steps:** 1 "Sit tall, arms straight" · 2 "Pull handle to belly" · 3 "Hold one second" · 4 "Reach
  forward slowly".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Rocking the torso** — torso leans forward to ~30° on the return and back to ~-20° on the pull.
     Metric: **torso lean change within the rep**, good ≤10°, bad ≥30°. Now: lower back and momentum
     do the work (same pattern as Fenwick 2009's bent-over row lumbar load). Later: heavier stacks
     with the same back size. Cue: "Torso still, arms move."
  2. **Shrugging the shoulders** — shoulders rise 3-5 cm toward the ears at the end of the pull.
     Metric: **acromion height change during the pull**, good ≤2 cm, bad ≥4 cm. Now: upper traps
     take over from mid traps/lats. Later: tight upper traps, little mid-back development. Cue:
     "Shoulders down, elbows back."
  3. **Short pull** — handle stops 10-15 cm in front of the belly, elbow ~120°. Metric: **handle-to-
     navel distance at the end**, good ≤5 cm, bad ≥12 cm. Now: shoulder blades never fully retract.
     Later: weaker mid-back. Cue: "Handle to the belly button."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-12. Safety: never release the handle under tension; set the stack down first.

### 3.6 `sa_pulldown` — Straight-arm cable pulldown
- **Station:** dual cable tower, pulley at the top, straight bar (or rope).
- **Muscles:** Prime: lats, teres major (shoulder extension with a fixed elbow isolates them from the
  biceps). Helper: posterior deltoid, triceps long head (holds the elbow, Muyor 2022 found the
  highest triceps EMG here), pec major (sternal fibres assist extension from overhead). Stabiliser:
  core, erector spinae (hinge). Evidence: Muyor 2022 (higher lat and triceps EMG than the barbell
  pullover, n=20, 30% body mass).
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pulley to the top and clip in the straight bar. Set the pin light. Grip
  the bar palms down at shoulder width, step back one pace, hinge forward ~30°, arms straight in
  front at head height. Do the set. Walk forward, let the bar rise under control, release it against
  the tower.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Standing, feet hip width, knees 10-15° bent, torso hinge 20-30° from vertical, fixed. Grip 1x
    biacromial. Elbow bent 10-20° and **locked at that angle** (coaching convention; no study specifies
    the number).
  - Start: shoulder flexion ~160° (hands at forehead height), cable taut.
  - Pull: arms sweep down in an arc to the thighs: shoulder flexion 160° → ~0° (bar touches the
    front of the thighs), scapulae depress; ~160° of shoulder travel. Chest stays up.
  - Return 2-3 s along the same arc to the stretch overhead; scapulae elevate slightly. Tempo
    3-1-1-0 (slow eccentric, light load).
- **Steps:** 1 "Hinge, arms up front" · 2 "Sweep bar to thighs" · 3 "Squeeze lats" · 4 "Rise slowly
  to stretch".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Bending the elbows (turns into a pushdown)** — elbow goes from 165° to ~90° during the pull.
     Metric: **elbow angle change within the rep**, good ≤10°, bad ≥40°. Now: triceps push the bar;
     lats stop isolating. Later: no lat benefit from the exercise. Cue: "Elbows soft, keep them
     still."
  2. **Standing bolt upright** — hinge 0-5°, bar hits the thighs at shoulder flexion ~30° with the arms
     still in front. Metric: **torso hinge angle**, good 20-30°, bad ≤5°. Now: the last 30° of lat
     shortening is cut off. Later: weaker finish, no extra growth. Cue: "Lean forward, bar to the
     thighs."
  3. **Too heavy, body bobs** — the torso dips 15-20° on every pull. Metric: **torso lean change
     within the rep**, good ≤5°, bad ≥15°. Now: the stretched position is yanked. Later: tendon
     irritation at the front of the shoulder from bouncing out of the stretch. Cue: "Half the weight,
     double the control."
- **Beginner:** 2-3 x 12-15, light (you should feel the lats, not the arms). Safety: light loads;
  shoulder history → smaller range at the top.

### 3.7 `deadlift` — Conventional deadlift
- **Station:** deadlift platform, barbell on the floor (20 kg bar; 15 kg or lighter plates on
  blocks to standard bar height for beginners).
- **Muscles:** Prime: **glutes and hamstrings** (hip extension), erector spinae (holds and extends
  the spine; this is the "back" part). Helper: quadriceps (knee extension off the floor, Escamilla
  2002), lats (hold the bar against the body, SBS), upper/mid traps (isometric, hold the shoulders
  up and back at lockout), forearm flexors (grip). Evidence: Escamilla 2000/2001/2002; Stronger by
  Science "How to deadlift". It is in a back session because the erector spinae and traps work
  hardest here under the heaviest loads.
- **Real-world flow:** Stand at the bar with the feet hip width, bar 2-3 cm from the shins over the
  shoelaces (midfoot). Hinge, bend the knees and grip the bar just outside the shins, palms down
  (mixed grip only when the double-overhand grip fails). Chest up, shoulders slightly in front of the
  bar, arms straight. Pull: stand up with the bar sliding along the legs, lock out tall. Lower it by
  hinging back until the bar passes the knees, then bend the knees; plates touch the floor. Repeat.
  After the set, leave the bar on the platform (strip the plates and return them to the tree).
- **Animation spec:**
  - Stance 1x biacromial (Escamilla 2000: 32±8 cm), feet turned out ~15° (14±6°), grip 55±10 cm
    (≈1.3-1.5x biacromial), bar over midfoot 2-3 cm from the shins ("1-2 inches", SBS).
  - Start (liftoff): torso hinge ~65-70° from vertical [DERIVED from Escamilla 2001: trunk 21-26°
    above horizontal for national lifters], thigh ~45° above horizontal, knee ~110-125° **[COACHING
    CONSENSUS, no printed value found]**, hip ~45-60°, shins touching the bar, shoulders 2-5 cm in
    front of the bar, arms straight, scapulae depressed (lats on), spine neutral.
  - Pull: bar rises vertically; hips and shoulders rise at the **same rate** until the bar passes the
    knees (knee ~160° at knee-pass, shank 83° from horizontal, Escamilla 2001), then the hips drive
    forward to lockout: hip 180°, knee 180°, torso 0°, shoulders over the bar, no lean-back.
  - Lower: hinge first (hips back, bar slides down the thighs), knees bend once the bar passes them;
    2 s down. Tempo 2-0-1-0 (reset the brace each rep, Valsalva, SBS).
- **Steps:** 1 "Bar over midfoot, grip" · 2 "Chest up, pull floor away" · 3 "Hips through, stand
  tall" · 4 "Hinge back, lower".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Hips shoot up first** — hips rise 15-20 cm before the bar leaves the floor; torso goes from 65°
     to ~85° lean. Metric: **torso lean change between liftoff and knee-pass**, good ≤10°, bad ≥20°.
     Now: it turns into a stiff-leg lift with a longer back lever (SBS "jerking the bar"). Later: the
     lower back limits every set before the legs do. Cue: "Chest and hips rise together."
  2. **Rounded lower back** — lumbar flexion 20-30° at liftoff. Metric: **lumbar flexion angle at
     liftoff**, good ≤10°, bad ≥20°. Now: more shear load on the lumbar discs (SBS; Cholewicki 1991).
     Later: repeated heavy flexion is the pattern most lifters' back complaints come from. Cue: "Big
     breath, chest up, flat back."
  3. **Bar drifting forward** — bar is 10-15 cm in front of the midfoot at knee height. Metric:
     **horizontal bar-to-midfoot distance at knee-pass**, good ≤3 cm, bad ≥10 cm. Now: a longer lever
     for the hips and back (Escamilla 2001: bigger hip moment arm = more L4/L5 load). Later: slower
     progress and a sore back. Cue: "Drag the bar up the legs."
  4. (shown only as a note) **Lean-back at lockout** — lumbar hyperextension ≥15°. Cue: "Stand tall,
     stop."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 5 with the bar + light plates on blocks at standard height; learn the hinge with a
  10-20 kg load first. Safety: never round under load; mixed grip or straps instead of letting the
  bar roll; flat shoes.

### 3.8 `db_shrug` — Dumbbell shrug
- **Station:** dumbbell rack (standing beside it).
- **Muscles:** Prime: upper trapezius (Ekstrom 2003: shrug = highest upper-trap activity of 10
  exercises), levator scapulae (helper under the trap). Helper: none of note. Stabiliser: forearm
  flexors (grip), erector spinae. Evidence: Ekstrom 2003. Arms held ~10-20° out from the sides gave
  a little more upper trap than arms straight down in work summarised by Henselmans
  (https://mennohenselmans.com/4-myths-keeping-your-shoulders-small-instead-of-yoked/) **[secondary
  source, unverified]**.
- **Real-world flow:** Take a pair of dumbbells from the rack, turn to face away from the rack, stand
  feet hip width, dumbbells hanging at the sides, palms in. Do the set. Turn back and return the
  dumbbells to the rack.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Standing, torso 0-5°, knees soft, arms straight (elbow 175-180°), dumbbells at the sides, palms
    facing the thighs.
  - Shrug: scapulae elevate **straight up** 4-6 cm (acromion rises ~5 cm; adult scapular elevation
    range is roughly 5-6 cm) toward the ears; no rolling; head neutral, chin level.
  - Hold 1 s at the top, lower 2 s to a full "drop" (scapulae depressed). Tempo 2-1-1-1.
  - Range is small; animate the whole body still except the shoulder girdle.
- **Steps:** 1 "Stand tall, arms straight" · 2 "Shrug straight up" · 3 "Hold one second" · 4 "Lower
  all the way".
- **Mistakes:**
  1. **Rolling the shoulders** — the acromion traces a 5 cm forward-back circle. Metric: **horizontal
     (front-back) travel of the acromion during the rep**, good ≤1 cm, bad ≥4 cm. Now: the horizontal
     part works against nothing (gravity is vertical). Later: nothing dangerous, just wasted reps;
     some people get an irritated AC joint. Cue: "Straight up, straight down."
  2. **Bending the elbows / curling** — elbow goes 180° → ~140°. Metric: **elbow angle at the top**,
     good ≥170°, bad ≤150°. Now: biceps lift part of the weight. Later: elbow strain with heavy
     dumbbells. Cue: "Arms are hooks, not levers."
  3. **Head poking forward** — the ear moves 5-8 cm in front of the shoulder. Metric: **ear-to-
     acromion horizontal offset**, good ≤3 cm, bad ≥6 cm. Now: neck muscles take the strain. Later:
     neck ache after shrug days. Cue: "Chin level, eyes forward."
- **Beginner:** 2-3 x 12-15. Safety: do not jerk heavy dumbbells; the range is small, the tempo slow.

## 4. Today's plan — Back (14 hard sets)

| # | Exercise | Sets x reps | Rest | Why it is in the plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Conventional deadlift | 3 x 5 | 3 min | **Erector spinae** (and upper traps isometric) under the heaviest load; compound first while fresh |
| 2 | Pull-up (or lat pulldown if <5 reps) | 2 x max-2 (or 2 x 8-12) | 2 min | **Lats + teres major + lower traps** from the vertical pull (ACE 2018: best lat exercise) |
| 3 | Barbell bent-over row | 3 x 8 | 2 min | **Mid traps + rhomboids**, lats, rear delts; second-best lower-trap exercise (ACE 2018) |
| 4 | Seated cable row | 2 x 10-12 | 90 s | **Mid traps/rhomboids** again with the spine supported (Lehman 2004) |
| 5 | Straight-arm cable pulldown | 2 x 12-15 | 60 s | **Lats + teres major** without the biceps limiting (Muyor 2022) |
| 6 | Dumbbell shrug | 2 x 12-15 | 60 s | **Upper traps** through their full range (Ekstrom 2003) |

Total 14 sets. Parts covered: lats (2, 3, 4, 5), upper traps (1, 6), mid traps (3, 4), lower traps
(2, 3), rhomboids (3, 4), teres major (2, 5), erector spinae (1, 3). Two sessions a week at this volume
sits at the low end of the 10-20 sets/week range (Schoenfeld 2017).

**Alternative plan — no barbell (dumbbells, cables, machines only), 13 sets:**
lat pulldown 3 x 8-12 (lats, teres major, lower traps) · one-arm dumbbell row 3 x 10 each side (lats,
mid traps/rhomboids, rear delts) · seated cable row 3 x 10-12 (mid traps/rhomboids) · straight-arm
pulldown 2 x 12-15 (lats) · dumbbell shrug 2 x 12-15 (upper traps). Erector spinae only work
isometrically here (hinge in the one-arm row and straight-arm pulldown); say so on screen.

## 6. Changelog and open questions

**Changes to the proposed list:** none. All eight exercises kept. Notes on choices:
- Seated cable row is specified with the **close neutral handle** (narrow grip = more lat, Padovan
  2025b) because the plan already has a wide-elbow row (barbell). If the owner wants the seated row
  to be the "mid-trap" exercise instead, use the wide bar (Padovan 2025b: more traps).
- Deadlift kept as "back" because of erector spinae/traps, but the site must list glutes and
  hamstrings as prime movers, not helpers.
- Pull-up: beginners cannot do it; the plan swaps in the lat pulldown below 5 reps.

**Open questions for the owner / 3D team:**
1. Deadlift start knee angle: no study printed an absolute knee angle at liftoff for the conventional
   deadlift (Escamilla reports thigh/trunk segment angles and group differences). I give 110-125° as
   coaching consensus; the 3D model should derive it from thigh 45° above horizontal, shin near
   vertical and limb lengths, and we report the derived number.
2. Bent-over row hinge angle (45°) is coaching consensus; the only study (Fenwick 2009) used one
   unstated angle. Fine for v1 if labelled as such.
3. Rhomboids cannot be measured with surface EMG; the site should light them *with* the mid traps and
   never quote a rhomboid-specific number.
4. Teres major: no exercise-specific EMG found; lit as a lat helper.
5. One-arm dumbbell row: a 2024 unilateral-vs-bilateral bent-over row EMG study exists (UiTM
   record 2-s2.0-85189340484) but the results were not retrievable; no claim about "one-arm = more
   lat" should appear.
6. Youdas 2010 numbers come from a summary of the paper, not the tables; the ranges are consistent
   across two independent summaries (Breaking Muscle, Europe PMC abstract) but should be re-checked
   if the owner wants them on screen.
7. Shrug "5 cm" elevation is an anatomical range, not from a shrug study; show "shrug as high as you
   can, straight up" rather than a number.
