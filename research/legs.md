# Legs knowledge base — "How to train your muscles" v1

Research date: 25 Oct 2026 (Fable). Same conventions as `chest-research.md`: every number that
reaches the animation or the screen traces to a source below. **[DISAGREEMENT]** = sources differ,
**[COACHING CONSENSUS]** = no measured data, only what good coaches agree on, **[gap]** = nothing found.

Angle conventions for the 3D team (used in every spec and every mistake metric):
- **Hip angle** = internal angle between the thorax line (shoulder→hip) and the thigh (hip→knee).
  180° standing tall; ~65° at the bottom of a deep squat.
- **Knee angle** = internal angle thigh→shank. 180° straight. "Knee flexion" = 180° minus this.
- **Ankle angle** = shank vs foot sole. 90° neutral; dorsiflexion = smaller than 90°.
- **Torso angle** = thorax line from vertical, 0° upright, 90° horizontal.
- **FPPA (knee valgus)** = frontal-plane angle at the knee between the hip→knee and knee→ankle
  lines; 0° straight, positive = knee inside the hip-ankle line (valgus). Wyndow et al. 2016,
  healthy adults, single-leg squat: peak FPPA **4° ± 7°** (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4727299/).
- **Tempo** = seconds down / pause / up / pause. Default **2-0-1-0**, as in chest (Schoenfeld 2015:
  0.5-8 s per rep all grow muscle alike).
- Legend as in chest: **Prime mover** (coral) / **Helper** (amber) / **Stabiliser** (dim).

## 1. Leg anatomy (the parts the site lights up)

| Part | Everyday name | What it does | Biased by (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quadriceps – vasti** (vastus lateralis, medialis, intermedius) | "Outer / inner / deep quad" | Straighten the knee. Cross only the knee. | Any knee-bending lift: squat, leg press, split squat, leg extension. Vasti grow the same from half or full squats (Kubo 2019, ~4.6-4.9%). |
| **Quadriceps – rectus femoris** | "Middle quad" (the strip down the front) | Straightens the knee **and** flexes the hip (crosses both). In squats it is slack, so it barely works. | **Leg extension**, best with the seat reclined to ~40° hip flexion (Larsen 2024). Squats did **not** grow it in 10 weeks (Kubo 2019); knee-extension training did (Ema 2016 lineage). |
| **Hamstrings** (biceps femoris long head, semitendinosus, semimembranosus; biceps femoris short head crosses only the knee) | "Back of thigh" | Extend the hip and bend the knee. Two-joint, so a lift must either hinge at the hip under load or curl the knee. | Hip hinge → **Romanian deadlift** (hip-dominant, upper hamstring); knee curl → **lying leg curl** (lower hamstring; Schoenfeld 2015). Squats grow them <1% (Kubo 2019). |
| **Gluteus maximus** | "Glutes / butt" | Extends the hip (standing up from a squat, locking out a hip thrust), posterior pelvic tilt, external rotation. | Deep squat (full > half: +6.7% vs +2.2%, Kubo 2019), **hip thrust** (highest glute EMG, Contreras 2015; grows the glutes as much as squats, Plotkin 2023), RDL. |
| **Gluteus medius** (+ minimus) | "Side hip / hip stabiliser" | Abducts the hip and stops the pelvis dropping and the knee caving on one leg. | Single-leg work: **Bulgarian split squat** (gluteus medius higher than in the back squat: McCurdy 2010 summarised by secondary sources **[unverified primary]**; 55% MVIC in RFESS, Norwegian thesis). |
| **Adductors** (adductor magnus is a big hip extensor when the hip is deeply flexed) | "Inner thigh" | Pull the thigh in; adductor magnus extends the hip from the deep squat. | Deep squat (+6.2% vs +2.7%, Kubo 2019), wide stance (McCaw & Melrose 1999 adductor longus). Shown as **helper** only; not a separate exercise. |
| **Gastrocnemius** (two heads) | "Upper calf / the diamond" | Points the foot down; crosses the knee so it works best with the **knee straight**. | **Standing calf raise** (+9-12% vs +0.6-1.7% seated; Kinoshita 2023). |
| **Soleus** | "Lower / deep calf" | Points the foot down; one joint, works at any knee angle; biggest of the three. | **Seated calf raise** takes the gastroc out (gastroc EMG falls from 30° knee flexion), **but** standing grows the soleus just as well (2.1% vs 2.9%, Kinoshita 2023). |

Helpers lit on every leg lift: erector spinae (holds the torso), core (brace), calves (ankle).

| **NSCA Kinetic Select, RDL** (https://www.nsca.com/education/articles/kinetic-select/romanian-deadlift-rdl/) | Technique | Knees slightly flexed and fixed; hips back, shoulders over or in front of the bar; bar close to the body; lower to just below the knee / tibial tuberosity or until neutral spine is lost. |

| **IPF depth rule** (secondary quotes, e.g. https://powerliftingtechnique.com/powerlifting-squat-technique-rules/) | Depth | "Top surface of the legs at the hip joint lower than the top of the knees". |

## 2. The exercises (9)

Beginner default unless stated: 3 x 8-12, 2-3 reps in reserve, 2 leg sessions a week, add the
smallest plate when every set hits 12 clean reps.

### 2.1 Barbell back squat (high bar)
- **Key:** `back_squat`. **Station:** power rack, J-hooks, barbell.
- **Parts:** Prime: vasti, glute max. Helper: adductor magnus, hamstrings (small; isometric), erectors, calves. Rectus femoris barely (Kubo 2019). Evidence: Kubo 2019, Caterisano 2002, Glassbrook 2017, Fry 2003.
- **Real-world flow:** J-hooks set at mid-chest height so the bar is unracked with a small knee bend. Walk in, duck under, bar on the upper traps (high bar: on the shelf just below C7, not on the neck), hands a little wider than shoulders, elbows down. Squeeze shoulder blades, big breath, stand up to lift the bar off the hooks. Two short steps back (left, right), set the feet, brace. After the last rep walk forward until the bar touches both uprights, then bend the knees to set it in the hooks. Never look for the hooks by leaning back.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Stance: feet shoulder-width (heels at biacromial width, 1.0-1.2x), toes out **15-30°** **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**; Escamilla 2001: foot angle does not change muscle activity.
  - Bar over the midfoot the whole rep; bar path vertical (±2 cm).
  - Start: hip 180°, knee 180°, ankle 90°, torso 0-5°.
  - Bottom (heavy high bar, Glassbrook 2019 data): hip internal **~65°**, knee internal **~55-60°** (knee flexion ~120-125°), ankle ~60° (≈30° dorsiflexion), torso **~35-45°** from vertical.
  - Depth rule to show on screen: **hip crease below the top of the knee** (IPF). Knees travel forward past the toes by about **5-10 cm** on this stance; that is normal (Fry 2003).
  - Knees track over the 2nd toe (FPPA 0-10°). Heels down. Neutral spine; the lumbar curve does not change by more than ~10° at the bottom.
  - Tempo 2-0-1-0; inhale and brace at the top, exhale past the sticking point.
- **Steps:** 1 Unrack, two steps back. 2 Brace, sit down between heels. 3 Hip crease below knee. 4 Drive up, breathe out.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Knees cave in* — both knees drift inward ~8 cm, FPPA ~20°. **Metric: FPPA at the bottom; good ≤10°, bad ≥15°.** Now: the adductors take over and the glute med is not doing its job. Later: repeated valgus under load is linked with patellofemoral pain and ACL risk in jumping sports; it mainly means the glutes are not driving. Fix: "Push the knees out over toes."
  2. *Good-morning squat* — hips shoot up first, torso tips to ~70°, bar drifts forward of the midfoot. **Metric: torso angle at the bottom; good 35-45°, bad ≥60°** (or bar 5 cm in front of midfoot). Now: load moves from quads to lower back; Fry 2003 shows hip torque multiplies when the knees stop going forward. Later: lower-back fatigue limits the squat; quads under-trained. Fix: "Chest up, knees forward together."
  3. *Half squat* — stops with thigh 20° above parallel. **Metric: hip crease height vs top of knee; good below, bad ≥5 cm above.** Now: glutes and adductors miss their hardest range (Caterisano 2002). Later: half squats grow glutes a third as much (Kubo 2019). Fix: "Sit lower, hips below knees."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 6-10, start with the empty bar (20 kg); set the safeties at the height of the bar at the bottom. Safety: collars on, safeties set, never rerack by leaning back.

### 2.2 Bulgarian split squat (dumbbells)
- **Key:** `bulgarian_split_squat`. **Station:** flat bench + dumbbell rack.
- **Parts:** Prime: vasti, glute max (front leg). Helper: **glute med** (single-leg balance), hamstrings (more than in a squat: Andersen 2014), adductors, obliques. Rectus femoris less than in a squat. Evidence: Andersen 2014, Arakawa 2025, Aygun-Polat 2025, McCurdy 2010 (secondary).
- **Real-world flow:** Pick two dumbbells from the rack, carry them hanging at the sides. Stand with the back to the bench, about one leg-length in front of it (lunge test: lunge forward until the front shin is vertical, then place the rear foot). Put the rear foot on the bench, laces down, shoelaces on the pad. Settle, then start. Switch legs after the set. Set the dumbbells on the floor, then return them to the rack.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Bench top at **~40-45 cm** (knee height). Front toe to bench edge **100-130% of leg length** (Arakawa 2025). Rear knee a hand's width above the floor at the bottom.
  - Torso **10-20°** forward (Arakawa natural lean ≈13°; more lean = more glute/hamstring, Aygun-Polat 2025).
  - Front knee: 180° → **~90°** at the bottom; front hip 180° → ~80°. Front shin ends near vertical to ~20° forward. Rear hip extends to ~160-170°.
  - Dumbbells hang straight under the shoulders; hips square to the front (pelvis rotation <10°).
  - Tempo 2-0-1-0. Depth marker: front thigh parallel or rear knee ~5 cm off the floor.
- **Steps:** 1 Rear foot on the bench. 2 Lower straight down, torso forward. 3 Rear knee near the floor. 4 Push through front foot up.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Front knee caves* — knee drops 6-8 cm inside the foot, FPPA ~20°. **Metric: front-knee FPPA at the bottom; good ≤10°, bad ≥15°.** Now: glute med gives up, balance goes. Later: knee pain with volume. Fix: "Knee over the second toe."
  2. *Foot too close* — toe-to-bench <80% leg length; front knee goes 15+ cm past the toe and the heel lifts. **Metric: front heel height at the bottom; good 0 cm, bad ≥2 cm.** Now: all knee, no hip; wobbly. Later: knee-dominant, glutes miss out. Fix: "Step further out, heel down."
  3. *Hips twist open* — pelvis rotates 20°+ toward the rear leg. **Metric: pelvis yaw vs front foot; good ≤10°, bad ≥20°.** Now: the glute med stops stabilising. Later: uneven load side to side. Fix: "Hips square, face forward."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 8-10 per leg, bodyweight first; dumbbells only when 10 clean reps balance easily. Safety: a stable, non-rolling bench; don't rush the switch.

### 2.3 Leg press (45° sled)
- **Key:** `leg_press`. **Station:** 45° leg press.
- **Parts:** Prime: vasti, glute max (more with feet high: Da Silva 2008). Helper: adductors, hamstrings (small), calves. Rectus femoris: more with feet low. Evidence: Escamilla 2001, Da Silva 2008.
- **Real-world flow:** Sit, buttocks and lower back on the pad, head on the headrest. Feet on the platform shoulder-width, mid-plate height, toes slightly out. Press the sled up ~5 cm to take the weight, flip both safety handles outward (release), grip the side handles. Lower under control, press up, stop short of a locked knee. Last rep: press up, flip the safeties back in, let the sled rest, then stand out.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Back pad ~45° to horizontal; feet at the middle of the platform, heels at shoulder width, toes out 10-20°.
  - Start (sled up): knee **~170°** (never 180°), hip ~100°.
  - Bottom: knee **~90°** (deeper if the lower back stays on the pad; coaching range 80-90°), hip **~70°**; the pelvis does not roll off the pad.
  - Sled travel ~35-45 cm along the rails. Knees track over the toes, FPPA 0-10°. Heels stay on the plate.
  - Tempo 2-0-1-0. Breathe out on the press.
- **Steps:** 1 Feet mid-plate, release safeties. 2 Lower until knees 90°. 3 Keep back on the pad. 4 Press up, knees soft.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Tailbone lifts* — going so deep the pelvis rolls, lower back rounds off the pad ~5 cm. **Metric: sacrum-to-pad gap at the bottom; good 0 cm, bad ≥3 cm.** Now: the lumbar spine flexes under the sled load. Later: the common source of leg-press back strain. Fix: "Stop where your butt stays down."
  2. *Locked knees* — knees snap to 180° at the top with the sled's weight on the joint. **Metric: knee angle at the top; good ≤172°, bad 180°.** Now: tension drops off the quads. Later: hyperextension stress on the knee. Fix: "Stop just short of straight."
  3. *Heels lift / knees cave* — heels off the plate 2-3 cm, knees inward FPPA 15-20°. **Metric: heel contact (good 0 cm gap, bad ≥2 cm).** Now: the calves and knees take the load. Later: knee irritation. Fix: "Drive through the whole foot."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-12; the sled itself may be 40-70 kg; learn the safety handles first. Safety: never unlock the safeties with the knees locked and the hands off the handles.

### 2.4 Leg extension
- **Key:** `leg_extension`. **Station:** leg extension machine.
- **Parts:** Prime: **rectus femoris** (the one quad the squat does not grow: Kubo 2019; grows most with the back pad reclined so the hip is ~40° flexed: Larsen 2024), vasti. Helper: none. Evidence: Ema 2016, Larsen 2024, Kubo 2019.
- **Real-world flow:** Sit; slide the back pad so the knee joint lines up with the machine's pivot axis (the red dot on the arm). Set the ankle pad on the lowest part of the shin, just above the ankle. Set the start lever so the knees begin at ~90°. Hold the side handles, back on the pad, buttocks down. Extend to straight, lower with control. After the set lower the stack gently, hook the pad, stand up.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Back pad reclined so hip angle is **~130-140°** (Larsen 2024 "40° hip flexion") rather than 90°. If the site only has one state, use the reclined one and say why.
  - Knee: start **~90°** (deeper, 80°, if the machine allows), end **~175-180°**.
  - Ankle pad on the distal shin; feet straight ahead; no toe pointing.
  - Pivot alignment: knee axis within 2 cm of the machine axis.
  - Tempo 1-1-2-0 (squeeze 1 s at the top, 2 s down). Buttocks stay on the seat.
- **Steps:** 1 Knee lined with the pivot. 2 Pad above the ankles. 3 Kick straight, squeeze one second. 4 Lower slowly, don't drop.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Hips lift off the seat* — buttocks rise 5 cm and the torso rocks back 15° to swing the weight. **Metric: hip-to-seat gap during the rep; good 0 cm, bad ≥3 cm.** Now: momentum, not quads. Later: no progress, knee pad bruises. Fix: "Butt down, grip the handles."
  2. *Weight stack slams* — the pad drops in <0.5 s and the plates clank. **Metric: eccentric duration; good ≥1.5 s, bad ≤0.5 s.** Now: half the rep (the lowering) is wasted. Later: slower growth. Fix: "Lower for two seconds."
  3. *Half reps* — stops at 140°. **Metric: knee angle at the top; good ≥170°, bad ≤150°.** Now: the top squeeze (where the vasti peak) is missing. Later: less rectus femoris growth. Fix: "All the way straight."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-15; best first quad exercise. Safety: no knee pain at full extension; if the knee cap hurts, shorten the top 20° (e3rehab).

### 2.5 Romanian deadlift (barbell)
- **Key:** `romanian_deadlift`. **Station:** power rack J-hooks at thigh height (or the deadlift platform, bar lifted from the floor once).
- **Parts:** Prime: **hamstrings** (hip-dominant: upper hamstring, biceps femoris highest here in McAllister 2014), **glute max**. Helper: erector spinae (isometric), adductor magnus, lats/grip. Evidence: NSCA RDL, McAllister 2014, Schoenfeld 2015.
- **Real-world flow:** Bar in the J-hooks at upper-thigh height. Grip just outside the thighs (double overhand), stand up to unrack, two steps back. Feet hip-width, toes forward. Shoulders back, bar against the thighs. Hinge: push the hips back, the bar slides down the thighs to just below the knee, stand up by driving the hips forward. Last rep: step in, set the bar in the hooks (knees bent, bar on the hooks, release).
- **Animation spec:**
  - Stance hip-width (heels ~25-30 cm apart), toes forward.
  - Knee: **~165-170°** (10-15° bend) and **fixed** for the whole rep **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**; NSCA: "slightly flexed, remain in this position".
  - Hip: 180° → **~90-100°** at the bottom; torso 0° → **~70-80°** from vertical (near horizontal) — ends where the hamstrings pull tight or the spine would round, whichever first.
  - Bar path: straight down the front of the thighs, in contact, to **just below the kneecap / top of the shin** (NSCA: tibial tuberosity); shins stay vertical; the bar stays over the midfoot.
  - Shoulders stay over or slightly in front of the bar. Neutral spine: lumbar angle change ≤10°.
  - Tempo 3-0-1-0 (slow lowering is where this lift works). Breathe in at the top, out as you stand.
- **Steps:** 1 Unrack, bar on thighs. 2 Hips back, knees soft. 3 Bar to just below knees. 4 Hips forward, stand tall.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Rounded back* — the lumbar curve flips: 20-25° of flexion at the bottom, shoulders slump. **Metric: lumbar flexion change from standing; good ≤10°, bad ≥20°.** Now: the spine, not the hamstrings, takes the stretch. Later: the classic deadlift-family back strain. Fix: "Chest proud, hinge at the hips."
  2. *Squatting it* — knees bend to 120°, hips drop, bar drifts 10 cm forward of the shins. **Metric: knee angle at the bottom; good ≥160°, bad ≤140°.** Now: it turns into a quad lift. Later: the hamstrings never get the stretch that grows them. Fix: "Knees stay soft, not bent."
  3. *Bar drifts forward* — the bar floats 8-10 cm in front of the thighs. **Metric: bar-to-thigh gap; good ≤3 cm, bad ≥8 cm.** Now: the lower back works at a long lever. Later: the back fatigues before the hamstrings. Fix: "Drag the bar down your legs."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 8-10 with the empty bar or light dumbbells; learn the hinge with a stick along the spine first. Safety: stop the lowering when the back wants to round; never bounce at the bottom.

### 2.6 Lying leg curl
- **Key:** `lying_leg_curl`. **Station:** lying (prone) leg curl machine.
- **Parts:** Prime: hamstrings, knee-dominant: **lower** hamstring region (Schoenfeld 2015) and **biceps femoris short head** (the only hamstring that does not cross the hip; grows the same prone or seated, Maeo 2021). Helper: gastrocnemius, glute max (isometric). **[DISAGREEMENT]:** the gym has a lying, not a seated, curl; Maeo 2021 shows the seated (hip 90°) version grows the two-joint hamstrings more (+14.1% vs +9.3%). We keep the lying curl because that is the machine the gym owns and the RDL supplies the long-length work; say so on the site. Evidence: Maeo 2021, Schoenfeld 2015.
- **Real-world flow:** Lie face down, hips on the bend of the pad, knees just past the edge of the bench so the kneecap hangs free; adjust the ankle roller to sit on the back of the ankles (Achilles), not the calves. Grip the handles, chest on the pad. Curl the heels toward the buttocks, lower slowly. After the set, lower fully, slide off sideways.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Body prone, hip ~**150°** on the angled pad (equivalent to the prone 30° hip flexion in Maeo 2021).
  - Knee: start **~175°** → top **~60-70°** (heels ~10 cm from the buttocks), back to 175°.
  - Ankle roller on the Achilles; feet neutral or slightly dorsiflexed (keeps the gastroc from stealing the rep).
  - Hips stay on the pad (hip angle changes ≤10°). Knees aligned with the machine pivot (±2 cm).
  - Tempo 1-1-2-0 (squeeze at the top, 2 s down).
- **Steps:** 1 Knees over the pad edge. 2 Roller behind the ankles. 3 Curl heels to the bum. 4 Lower slowly, don't drop.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Hips lift* — the buttocks rise 5-8 cm off the pad and the back arches to cheat. **Metric: hip angle change during the rep; good ≤10°, bad ≥20°** (or hip-to-pad gap ≥3 cm). Now: the hip flexors and back do the lift. Later: lower-back ache, hamstrings under-loaded. Fix: "Hips glued to the pad."
  2. *Half curl* — stops at 120°. **Metric: knee angle at the top; good ≤75°, bad ≥110°.** Now: no peak contraction. Later: slower growth. Fix: "Heels all the way up."
  3. *Dropping the weight* — eccentric <0.5 s, roller bounces. **Metric: eccentric duration; good ≥1.5 s, bad ≤0.5 s.** Now: half the stimulus lost. Later: fewer gains, roller bruises. Fix: "Two seconds on the way down."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-15. Safety: pad over the Achilles, not the calf; no knee hyperextension at the bottom (stop at 175°).

### 2.7 Barbell hip thrust
- **Key:** `hip_thrust`. **Station:** flat bench (or the lowest step) beside the dumbbell area, barbell with a pad, plates on the floor.
- **Parts:** Prime: **glute max** (highest glute EMG of any lift here, Contreras 2015; grows the glutes as much as squats, Plotkin 2023). Helper: hamstrings (40.8% MVIC), vasti (99.5% MVIC — the quads work hard), adductors. Evidence: Contreras 2015, Plotkin 2023, Contreras guide.
- **Real-world flow:** Bench against the rack or a wall so it cannot slide. Sit on the floor with the upper back against the bench edge, roll the padded bar over the legs to the hip crease (large plates make this possible). Feet flat, shoulder-width, heels ~30-40 cm from the buttocks. Hands on the bar, chin tucked, drive the hips up until the torso is level, squeeze, lower. After the set lower the hips to the floor, roll the bar forward off the hips.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Bench top **~35-40 cm** (Contreras: 12-15 in, up to 16 in for tall). Bench contacts the back just below the shoulder blades (the bra-strap line); the upper back hinges on the edge.
  - Bottom: hips ~5 cm off the floor, hip angle ~100°, knee ~80-90°.
  - Top: **torso horizontal**, hip angle **~180°** (no more), **knee 90°**, **shins vertical** (Contreras); feet flat, toes slightly out.
  - Head/chin: chin stays tucked, eyes on the knees; ribs down (no lumbar arch: lumbar angle change ≤10°).
  - Bar sits in the hip crease, moves in a vertical arc (hinge at the shoulders). Tempo 1-1-1-1 (1 s squeeze at the top).
- **Steps:** 1 Upper back on the bench. 2 Bar in the hip crease. 3 Drive hips up, chin tucked. 4 Squeeze on top, lower slow.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Arched lower back* — the lift finishes by arching: hips go past 180° to ~195° and the ribs flare, head drops back. **Metric: hip angle at the top; good 175-180°, bad ≥190°.** Now: the lumbar spine, not the glutes, closes the last inch. Later: low-back ache, glutes never fully squeezed. Fix: "Ribs down, squeeze, no arch."
  2. *Feet too far out* — shins lean 25°+ forward at the top, heels lift. **Metric: shin angle from vertical at the top; good ≤10°, bad ≥25°.** Now: hamstrings take over; tiptoes. Later: hamstring cramp, less glute. Fix: "Shins straight up at the top."
  3. *Half thrust* — the hips stop 10 cm below level, hip angle ~150°. **Metric: hip angle at the top; good ≥175°, bad ≤155°.** Now: the glute's hardest range (full extension) is skipped. Later: less growth. Fix: "Hips all the way up."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-12, start with the empty bar (pad!) or bodyweight. Safety: pad on the bar, bench anchored, large plates so the bar rolls over the legs.

### 2.8 Standing calf raise (machine)
- **Key:** `standing_calf_raise`. **Station:** standing calf raise machine (shoulder pads, step).
- **Parts:** Prime: **gastrocnemius** (knee straight; +9-12% vs ~1% seated, Kinoshita 2023), **soleus** (grows the same here as seated, +2.1% vs +2.9%). Helper: none. Evidence: Kinoshita 2023, Kassiano 2023, Nunes 2020.
- **Real-world flow:** Set the shoulder pads so you must bend the knees slightly to get under them. Step onto the platform, balls of the feet on the edge, heels hanging, feet hip-width and straight. Stand up to lift the pads (this unlocks the stack; some machines have a release lever at the side). Lower the heels until the calves are fully stretched, rise onto the toes, hold a second. After the set bend the knees to set the stack down (or push the lever) and step off backwards.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Knees **straight (175-180°)** and fixed. Torso upright, hips under the shoulders.
  - Ankle: bottom **~65°** (≈25° dorsiflexion, heels below the step edge), top **~115°** (≈25° plantar flexion, on the balls of the feet) = the full -25…+25° range of Kassiano 2023; **pause 1 s at the bottom** (the stretch is where it grows: +15% vs +7% for the top half).
  - Feet straight ahead, hip-width; weight on the big-toe side of the ball of the foot.
  - Heel travel ~12-15 cm. Tempo 2-1-1-1.
- **Steps:** 1 Balls of feet on the edge. 2 Heels down, feel the stretch. 3 Rise high on your toes. 4 Hold, then lower slowly.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Bouncing* — fast rebound off the Achilles at the bottom, rep time <1 s, heels never pause. **Metric: time at the bottom 10° of range; good ≥0.5 s, bad ≤0.1 s.** Now: the tendon, not the muscle, does the work. Later: calves don't grow; Achilles irritation. Fix: "Pause at the bottom, no bounce."
  2. *Half range* — heels never drop below the step: ankle only 90°→110°. **Metric: ankle angle at the bottom; good ≤70°, bad ≥85°.** Now: misses the stretched position (Kassiano 2023). Later: half the growth. Fix: "Heels below the step."
  3. *Knees bend* — knees flex to 150° to help. **Metric: knee angle; good ≥170°, bad ≤155°.** Now: the gastroc goes slack (Korean EMG: from 30° knee flexion). Later: it quietly becomes a soleus exercise. Fix: "Legs straight, calves only."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-15 with a slow stretch. Safety: hold the handles; don't let the heels drop fast under heavy load.

### 2.9 Seated calf raise (soleus)
- **Key:** `seated_calf_raise`. **Station:** seated calf raise machine (knee pads).
- **Parts:** Prime: **soleus** (knee at 90° takes the gastroc off: gastroc EMG falls from 30° knee flexion; soleus unchanged). Helper: gastrocnemius (small). **Honest note for the site:** standing raises grow the soleus just as well (Kinoshita 2023), so this is the *soleus-biased option*, not a must. Evidence: Kinoshita 2023, Korean EMG 2018.
- **Real-world flow:** Sit, balls of the feet on the step, heels hanging. Pull the knee pad down so it sits on the lower thighs just above the knees (adjust the pin). Hold the pad handle, rise onto the toes to lift the pad, push the stop lever forward to release. Lower heels, rise, repeat. Last rep: rise, pull the stop back, lower the pad, slide the knees out.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Hip ~90°, **knee 90°** (fixed), torso upright, hands on the pad.
  - Ankle: bottom **~65-70°** (heels well below the step), top **~115°**; pause 1 s at the bottom.
  - Feet straight, hip-width. Heel travel ~10-12 cm. Tempo 2-1-1-1.
- **Steps:** 1 Pad just above the knees. 2 Heels drop below the step. 3 Rise onto the toes. 4 Pause low, pause high.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Bouncing* — as 2.8; **metric: time at the bottom 10°; good ≥0.5 s, bad ≤0.1 s.** Now: tendon rebound. Later: no growth. Fix: "Slow at the bottom."
  2. *Half range* — heels stay level with the step. **Metric: ankle at the bottom; good ≤72°, bad ≥85°.** Now: no stretch. Later: half the growth. Fix: "Heels all the way down."
  3. *Pad on the kneecap* — pad sits on the knee instead of the thigh (5 cm too far forward). **Metric: pad centre to kneecap distance; good ≥5 cm up the thigh, bad 0 cm.** Now: it hurts. Later: bruised kneecap, people quit the exercise. Fix: "Pad on the thigh, not the knee."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 12-20. Safety: release and re-engage the stop with the heels up, not down.

## 5. Changelog and open questions

**Changes to the proposed list**
- Kept all 9 (8 + seated calf raise). Reason to keep the 9th: the gym model owns the machine and
  it is the soleus-*biased* exercise; but the site must say standing raises grow the soleus just as
  well (Kinoshita 2023), so Plan A uses standing only and Plan B uses both. Owner's call whether 9 → 8.
- Lying leg curl kept although **seated** curls grow the two-joint hamstrings more (Maeo 2021):
  the gym has no seated curl, and the RDL covers the long-length work. Recommend adding a seated
  curl to the equipment list in v2 **[open question]**.
- Leg extension spec uses a **reclined** back pad (hip ~130-140°) because rectus femoris growth was
  far higher at 40° hip flexion (Larsen 2024, preprint, n=22). If the 3D machine only has an
  upright seat, say "lean back a little" in the text.
- Squat spec is **high bar** (upper traps, torso ~35-45°), matching the "straight back, deep"
  look; low bar is not shown.
- "Knees past toes" is explicitly **not** a mistake (Fry 2003); the squat mistake list uses knee
  cave, good-morning and half depth instead.

**Open questions for the owner**
1. FPPA threshold: healthy single-leg mean is 4° ± 7° (Wyndow 2016); we call ≥15° "caving".
   No loaded-bilateral-squat cut-off exists in the literature.
2. Leg-press depth: no study; 90° knee / "pelvis stays on the pad" is coaching consensus.
3. Hip-thrust bench height 35-40 cm comes from Contreras' blog, not a study; scale by model height
   (knee-height rule). Chin-tucked/ribs-down cues are coaching consensus.
4. McCurdy 2010 (Bulgarian > squat for glute med) was only seen in secondary summaries; the
   Norwegian thesis (55% MVIC) and Andersen 2014 are the verifiable numbers.
5. Bulgarian split squat torso lean: Arakawa 2025 natural lean ≈13°; Aygun-Polat 2025 says more
   lean = more glute/hamstring but gives no angle. Spec says 10-20°.
6. RDL knee bend "10-15°" is coaching consensus (NSCA says only "slightly flexed").
7. Beginner "3 x 8-12" and rest times are the same defaults as chest (Schoenfeld load/volume
   reviews), not leg-specific trials.
