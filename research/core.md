# Core knowledge base — "How to train your muscles" v1

Research date: 25 Oct 2026 (Fable). Conventions as in `chest-research.md` and `legs.md`:
**[DISAGREEMENT]**, **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**, **[gap]**. Angle conventions (hip, knee, torso,
lumbar) are the ones defined at the top of `legs.md`. Extra ones for the core:
- **Hip-line sag** = vertical distance of the hip joint below the straight shoulder→ankle line
  (plank) or shoulder→knee line (ab wheel). 0 cm = straight body.
- **Lumbar extension** = change of the lumbar curve from the neutral standing value; positive =
  more arch (hollow back), negative = flexion (rounding).
- **Pelvic tilt** = angle of the ASIS→PSIS line; posterior tilt = tailbone tucked under.
- Legend: **Prime mover** (coral) / **Helper** (amber) / **Stabiliser** (dim).
- Core work is mostly **holding** (anti-movement). Isometric holds are timed in seconds, not reps.

## 1. Core anatomy

| Part | Everyday name | What it does | Biased by (evidence) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rectus abdominis** (one muscle, tendinous bands make the "six-pack" segments) | "Abs / six-pack" | Flexes the spine (curls ribs toward pelvis **or** pelvis toward ribs); resists extension (keeps the back from arching in a plank or roll-out). | Spine flexion under load: **cable crunch**, **hanging knee raise** (pelvis curls up), **ab wheel** (anti-extension; highest RA in Escamilla 2006). |
| Rectus abdominis — "upper vs lower fibres" note | "Upper abs / lower abs" | Same muscle, segmental nerve supply. **[DISAGREEMENT]** Lehman & McGill 2001 (EMG, n=11): **no** difference between upper and lower portions in any exercise after normalisation. Sarti 1996 (n=33): people with good technique got **more lower-RA activity in the reverse curl** (pelvis-to-ribs). Gomirato & Grenier 2023 (ultrasound): crunch thickens the upper part more than a leg raise. Verdict for the site: "you can *bias* the lower part slightly by moving the pelvis instead of the ribs; you cannot isolate it". | Ribs-to-pelvis → crunch (upper bias); pelvis-to-ribs → hanging knee raise (lower bias, small). |
| **External oblique** | "Side abs (outer)" | Rotates the trunk to the **opposite** side, bends it sideways, posterior-tilts the pelvis, resists rotation. | **Side plank** (69% MVIC, Ekstrom 2007 secondary), **Pallof press** (anti-rotation), **ab wheel / hanging knee raise** (Escamilla 2006: highest EO). |
| **Internal oblique** | "Side abs (inner)" | Rotates the trunk to the **same** side, side-bends, compresses the abdomen. | Same as external oblique; highest in Power Wheel and hanging knee-up (Escamilla 2006). |
| **Transversus abdominis** | "Corset / deep abs" | Wraps the belly like a belt; raises intra-abdominal pressure; does **not** move the spine. Trained by **bracing** (stiffening the whole wall), not by "sucking in" — bracing gives +32% spine stability, the TrA alone ~0.14% (Grenier & McGill 2007). | Every brace: **dead bug**, **plank**, **Pallof**. Dead bug gave the largest TrA thickness increase among Pilates-type drills (2023 ultrasound). |
| **Erector spinae + multifidus** | "Lower-back muscles" | Extend the spine; in core drills they are the **anti-flexion partners** that co-contract with the abs so the trunk stays stiff (McGill's "bracing"). | Lit as **helper** in plank, side plank, Pallof, dead bug; **stabiliser** in cable crunch and ab wheel (Escamilla 2006: lumbar paraspinal EMG low and similar in all ab exercises; Marchetti 2015: no change across roll-out angles). The RDL and squat in `legs.md` are where they work hard. |
| Helpers that must be lit | Hip flexors (**rectus femoris, iliopsoas**) | Lift the thigh in hanging knee raise and dead bug; Escamilla 2006: rectus femoris highest in Power Wheel pike/knee-up, incline reverse crunch, bent-knee sit-up. | Show amber in hanging knee raise and dead bug; "if you only feel the front of the hips, curl the pelvis". |
| | **Glute max / glute med**, lats, pecs, serratus | Side plank: glute med **74% MVIC** (Ekstrom 2007, secondary). Ab wheel: pec major and lats hold the arms (Marchetti 2015). | Dim stabiliser state. |

Anatomy references: Kenhub (https://www.kenhub.com/en/library/anatomy/abdominal-muscles), Grenier &

## 2. The exercises (7)

Beginner default: 3 sets; holds 20-45 s or 8-15 controlled reps; 2-3 reps/seconds short of losing
form; 2-3 times a week. Breathe throughout holds (no breath holding beyond 2-3 s).

### 2.1 Front plank (forearm)
- **Key:** `front_plank`. **Station:** mat.
- **Parts:** Prime: rectus abdominis, external + internal oblique, transversus (anti-extension brace). Helper: glutes (posterior tilt), quads, serratus/pecs (shoulders). Stabiliser: erectors (low; Schoenfeld 2014). Evidence: Schoenfeld 2014, Ekstrom 2007 (<45% MVIC → an endurance drill), McGill 1999.
- **Real-world flow:** Kneel on the mat, forearms down, elbows under the shoulders, hands flat or fists. Step one foot back, then the other, toes tucked, feet hip-width. Squeeze the glutes, tuck the tailbone a little, hold. Finish by dropping the knees to the mat, sit back on the heels, stand up.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Elbows **directly under the shoulders** (standard) or **10-15 cm forward** of them (long-lever version, Schoenfeld 2014 — show as the "harder" variant).
  - Body one straight line: shoulder–hip–ankle, **hip-line sag 0 cm** (±2 cm). Torso angle from horizontal ~10-15° (shoulders higher than ankles because of forearm height).
  - Pelvis: slight **posterior tilt** (~10° from standing neutral), ribs down. Lumbar extension change **0° to -5°**.
  - Head neutral, eyes on the hands (neck in line). Feet hip-width.
  - Hold **20-45 s**; or McGill style 3-5 x 10 s with 3 s rests. Breathe normally.
- **Steps:** 1 Elbows under the shoulders. 2 Feet back, body straight. 3 Squeeze glutes, tuck tailbone. 4 Breathe, hold, stay straight.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Hips sag* — the hip line drops 8-10 cm below the shoulder-ankle line, lower back arches. **Metric: hip-line sag; good ≤2 cm, bad ≥6 cm** (or lumbar extension ≥+15°). Now: the abs let go, the lumbar spine hangs. Later: low-back ache after planks instead of ab work. Fix: "Squeeze glutes, tuck the tailbone."
  2. *Hips piked* — hips 10-15 cm above the line, body a tent. **Metric: hip-line height; good ≤+3 cm, bad ≥+8 cm.** Now: the abs are unloaded, the shoulders hold you. Later: no progress. Fix: "Lower the hips, long body."
  3. *Head drops / shoulders by the ears* — chin to chest 30°, shoulder blades collapse. **Metric: head-neck angle vs torso line; good ±10°, bad ≥25°.** Now: neck strain, serratus off. Later: neck ache. Fix: "Eyes on hands, push the floor."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 20-30 s (or from the knees), progress 5 s a week to 45-60 s, then move to the long-lever version rather than longer holds. Safety: stop when the hips drop; no breath holding.

### 2.2 Side plank
- **Key:** `side_plank`. **Station:** mat.
- **Parts:** Prime: **obliques** (external oblique 69% MVIC, top side), quadratus lumborum, **glute med** of the bottom leg (72-74% MVIC, the highest glute-med value in Ekstrom 2007's set). Helper: rectus abdominis, erectors (34-42%). Evidence: Ekstrom 2007, McGill 1999.
- **Real-world flow:** Lie on one side, elbow under the shoulder, forearm pointing forward, legs straight, feet stacked (or top foot in front of the bottom one for balance). Lift the hips until the body is straight, top hand on the hip or arm up. Hold, lower the hips, roll over, repeat on the other side.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Elbow **under the shoulder** (±3 cm), forearm perpendicular to the body.
  - Body straight: shoulder–hip–ankle in one line viewed from the front; **hip-line sag 0 cm** (±2 cm); torso rotation 0° (hips stacked, chest facing sideways, pelvis roll ≤10°).
  - Feet stacked or staggered (top foot 10 cm in front). Top arm on the hip or straight up.
  - Hold **15-40 s per side**; left and right within **5 s** of each other (McGill norms: side bridge symmetry).
- **Steps:** 1 Elbow under the shoulder. 2 Lift hips, body straight. 3 Hips stacked, don't roll. 4 Hold, breathe, switch sides.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Hips sag* — hip drops 6-10 cm toward the floor, body a banana. **Metric: hip-line sag (front view); good ≤2 cm, bad ≥6 cm.** Now: obliques and glute med stop working. Later: the drill does nothing. Fix: "Hips up, long straight line."
  2. *Rolling forward* — chest turns toward the floor, pelvis rolls 25-30°. **Metric: pelvis roll from vertical; good ≤10°, bad ≥25°.** Now: the load leaves the obliques for the shoulder. Later: shoulder ache. Fix: "Chest faces the wall, not the floor."
  3. *Shoulder shrugged* — the shoulder sits 5 cm toward the ear, elbow 10 cm in front of the shoulder line. **Metric: elbow-to-shoulder horizontal offset; good ≤3 cm, bad ≥10 cm.** Now: the shoulder joint is stressed. Later: sore front shoulder. Fix: "Push the floor away, elbow under."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 15-20 s per side, from the knees if needed. Safety: wrist/shoulder pain → do it from the knees.

### 2.3 Dead bug
- **Key:** `dead_bug`. **Station:** mat.
- **Parts:** Prime: rectus abdominis, obliques, **transversus** (brace; largest TrA thickening of the drills tested, 2023 ultrasound). Helper: hip flexors (rectus femoris, iliopsoas) of the moving leg, anterior deltoid. Stabiliser: erectors. Evidence: Souza 2001 (≤41% MVIC; control drill), Korean 2017 (both limbs > one), Pilates 2023.
- **Real-world flow:** Lie on the back, arms straight up to the ceiling, hips and knees at 90° (shins level, "table top"). Press the lower back into the mat (small posterior tilt), breathe out. Lower the right arm overhead and the left leg toward the floor at the same time, stop before the lower back lifts, return, switch. Finish: feet down, roll to the side, sit up.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Start: hip **90°**, knee **90°**, shoulders **90°** flexed (arms vertical). Lumbar flat: gap between lumbar spine and mat **0-2 cm** (a hand should not slide under).
  - Movement: opposite arm and leg. Arm to **~160-170°** overhead (hand ~5 cm off the floor). Leg: hip opens to **~135-150°** (heel **5-10 cm** off the mat), knee may stay 90° (easier) or straighten (harder).
  - The other arm/leg do not move (drift ≤5°). Pelvis does not tilt (change ≤5°). Ribs down.
  - Tempo 2-1-2-0 per side; exhale on the reach. 8-10 reps per side.
- **Steps:** 1 Lower back pressed down. 2 Opposite arm and leg reach. 3 Stop before the back lifts. 4 Return slowly, switch sides.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Lower back arches* — a gap opens under the lumbar spine as the leg goes down; lumbar extension +15-20°. **Metric: lumbar-to-mat gap; good ≤2 cm, bad ≥5 cm.** Now: the hip flexors win, the abs lose. Later: the drill becomes a hip-flexor exercise and the back gets cranky. Fix: "Only as low as the back stays down."
  2. *Too fast / both sides* — arm and leg swing in <1 s, the still limbs drift 20°. **Metric: movement time per side; good ≥3 s, bad ≤1 s.** Now: momentum, no bracing. Later: no learning of the brace. Fix: "Slow, like moving through honey."
  3. *Head and shoulders lift* — chin tucks hard, shoulders 5 cm off the mat, neck strains. **Metric: head-to-mat gap; good 0 cm, bad ≥4 cm.** Now: neck muscles, not abs. Later: neck ache. Fix: "Head heavy on the mat."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 6-8 per side, legs only first, then opposite arm and leg. Safety: this is the entry drill for anyone with a sore back; stop if the back lifts.

### 2.4 Hanging knee raise
- **Key:** `hanging_knee_raise`. **Station:** pull-up bar (hanging) or captain's chair (forearms on pads). The spec below is the captain's-chair version, which is the ACE "captain's chair" (2nd for RA, 1st for obliques); the bar version adds grip and lats.
- **Parts:** Prime: rectus abdominis (lower-bias when the pelvis curls: Sarti 1996), external + internal oblique (Escamilla 2006: hanging knee-up with straps among the highest for every ab muscle). Helper: **hip flexors** (rectus femoris, iliopsoas — large; this is why the pelvic curl matters), lats, grip. Evidence: Escamilla 2006, ACE 2001, Sarti 1996.
- **Real-world flow:** Step up onto the foot rests, forearms on the pads, grip the handles, back against the pad, step off so the legs hang. Start with a slight posterior tilt, draw the knees up and **curl the pelvis** so the lower back rounds off the pad, lower slowly. Finish: feet back on the rests, step down.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Start: hanging straight, hip **~175°**, knee ~175°, pelvis slightly posteriorly tilted, no swing.
  - Top: knees to chest height: hip **~70-80°**, knee **~90°**, pelvis **posteriorly tilted ~20-30°** from the start (tailbone comes forward, lumbar spine rounds ~15°) — this is the part that loads the abs rather than the hip flexors.
  - Shoulders down (no shrug), elbows under the shoulders on the pads. Torso sway ≤5 cm (no swinging).
  - Tempo 1-1-2-0 (1 s up, 1 s squeeze, 2 s down). 8-15 reps.
- **Steps:** 1 Hang tall, shoulders down. 2 Knees up to the chest. 3 Curl the pelvis, round low back. 4 Lower slowly, no swing.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Swinging* — torso pendulums 15-20 cm, reps use momentum. **Metric: horizontal hip sway per rep; good ≤5 cm, bad ≥15 cm.** Now: momentum, not abs. Later: no progress, grip tires first. Fix: "Pause at the bottom, no swing."
  2. *Hip-flexor lift (no curl)* — knees come up but the pelvis stays still: pelvic tilt change 0°, lower back flat or arched. **Metric: pelvic tilt change from start to top; good ≥15°, bad ≤5°.** Now: rectus femoris and iliopsoas do it all (Escamilla 2006 warns about this). Later: tight hip flexors, abs undertrained. Fix: "Curl the hips up, not just knees."
  3. *Shrugged shoulders* — shoulders ride up 5 cm toward the ears. **Metric: shoulder-to-ear distance change; good ≤2 cm, bad ≥5 cm.** Now: the neck and traps hold you. Later: neck strain. Fix: "Push the shoulders down."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 8-12 knee raises (captain's chair first, bar later); progress to straight legs. Safety: lower under control; if the lower back hurts, go back to the dead bug.

### 2.5 Kneeling cable crunch
- **Key:** `cable_crunch`. **Station:** cable tower, pulley at the top, rope attachment.
- **Parts:** Prime: rectus abdominis (loaded spinal flexion, whole length). Helper: obliques, lats (hold the rope still), hip flexors (should stay quiet). Evidence: **no EMG study found [gap]**; treated as the crunch (ACE baseline) with external load. All numbers **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**.
- **Real-world flow:** Set the pulley at the top, clip the rope. Kneel 30-50 cm in front of the tower facing it, knees hip-width, grab the rope ends and bring them to the sides of the head/under the chin. Hips stay still (about 30° short of straight), elbows point down. Crunch the ribs toward the pelvis until the elbows reach the thighs, return slowly until the abs are stretched. Finish: stand up with the rope, let the stack down gently, unclip.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Kneeling: knee 90°, hip **~150°** and **fixed** (the hips do not swing; hip-angle change ≤10°).
  - Hands at the temples, rope against the head, elbows pointing to the thighs.
  - Start: spine neutral-to-slightly extended, torso ~20° from vertical. Top: spine flexed **~40-50°** (thoracic+lumbar), elbows touch mid-thigh, forehead moves ~30 cm toward the knees.
  - The movement is **ribs toward pelvis**; the cable stays taut; the stack does not touch down between reps.
  - Tempo 1-1-2-0. 10-15 reps.
- **Steps:** 1 Rope by the head. 2 Hips still, elbows down. 3 Ribs crunch toward knees. 4 Return slow, abs stretched.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Hip hinge instead of crunch* — the torso folds at the hips (hip angle 150° → 90°) with a straight spine. **Metric: hip-angle change per rep; good ≤10°, bad ≥40°** (or spinal flexion change ≤10°). Now: hip flexors and lats move the weight. Later: abs grow nothing, hip flexors tighten. Fix: "Curl the spine, hips don't move."
  2. *Pulling with the arms* — hands drift 20 cm from the head, elbows straighten. **Metric: hand-to-head distance; good ≤5 cm, bad ≥15 cm.** Now: a lat pulldown. Later: nothing for the abs. Fix: "Rope glued to your head."
  3. *Sitting back on the heels* — the buttocks drop to the heels at the bottom (hip 150° → 70°). **Metric: hip angle at the top; good ≥140°, bad ≤100°.** Now: momentum from the hips. Later: no ab stimulus. Fix: "Stay tall on the knees."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 10-15 with a light stack (the first plate). Safety: kneel on a mat; don't let the stack yank you upright.

### 2.6 Pallof press (cable)
- **Key:** `pallof_press`. **Station:** cable tower, pulley at chest height, D-handle.
- **Parts:** Prime: **obliques** (anti-rotation; external oblique on the far side, internal oblique on the cable side), transversus (brace). Helper: rectus abdominis, erectors/multifidus (co-contract; the WKU pilot saw erector changes), glutes, anterior deltoid (press). Evidence: **no peer-reviewed Pallof EMG [gap]**; WKU pilot (n=4) only; the anti-rotation role follows from the obliques' anatomy **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**.
- **Real-world flow:** Pulley at chest height, D-handle. Stand side-on to the tower, step out 1-1.5 m until the cable is taut, feet shoulder-width, knees soft, handle held in both hands at the sternum. Press the hands straight out to arms' length, hold 2 s, bring them back. Do the set, turn around, repeat on the other side. Finish: step toward the tower, lower the stack, unclip.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Stance: feet shoulder-width, side-on to the tower, knees ~165°, torso 0° (upright), cable horizontal at sternum height.
  - Hands: start at the sternum (0 cm), press to **full reach (~55-65 cm)** straight ahead at sternum height, hold **2 s**, return. 8-12 reps per side.
  - Trunk rotation toward the tower during the press: **≤5°**; shoulders and hips stay square to the front (pelvis yaw ≤5°). Shoulder blades down.
  - Tempo 1-2-1-0. Exhale as the arms go out.
- **Steps:** 1 Side-on, cable taut. 2 Handle at the chest. 3 Press straight out, hold. 4 Don't twist, bring back.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Trunk twists* — shoulders rotate 20-30° toward the tower at full reach. **Metric: shoulder-line yaw vs start; good ≤5°, bad ≥20°.** Now: the cable wins, the obliques give up the fight. Later: the drill trains nothing. Fix: "Belly button faces front."
  2. *Leaning away* — torso side-bends 15° away from the tower to counterweight. **Metric: torso lateral lean; good ≤5°, bad ≥15°.** Now: bodyweight does the anti-rotation. Later: no oblique work. Fix: "Stand tall, stay centred."
  3. *Hands drift toward the tower* — the handle ends 15 cm off the midline. **Metric: hand offset from the sternum midline at full reach; good ≤5 cm, bad ≥15 cm.** Now: a shorter lever, easier. Later: less and less load. Fix: "Press straight from your sternum."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 8-10 per side with the lightest plate. Safety: light load; step in before releasing the handle.

### 2.7 Ab wheel rollout (from the knees)
- **Key:** `ab_wheel_rollout`. **Station:** mat + ab wheel.
- **Parts:** Prime: **rectus abdominis** (anti-extension; the Power Wheel roll-out was among the highest RA/oblique exercises in Escamilla 2006; RA activity doubles as the arms go out, Marchetti 2015), external + internal oblique. Helper: **lats and pec major** (hold the arms; pec +90% at the long position), triceps, hip flexors. Stabiliser: erectors (low, unchanged with reach). Evidence: Escamilla 2006, Marchetti 2015.
- **Real-world flow:** Kneel on the mat, wheel on the floor under the shoulders, hands on the handles, arms straight. Tuck the tailbone and squeeze the glutes. Roll the wheel forward, hips opening, until the arms are overhead and the body nearly straight from knees to hands, then pull the wheel back to the knees with the abs. Finish: sit back on the heels, put the wheel aside.
- **Animation spec:**
  - Start: kneeling, hip **~90°**, shoulders **~90°** flexed (wheel under the shoulders), arms straight (elbow 170-180°), spine neutral with a slight posterior tilt.
  - End: hip **~150-165°**, shoulders **~150-160°** flexed (arm-trunk 150°, the hardest position in Marchetti 2015), knee-hip-shoulder in one line, **hip-line sag ≤3 cm**, lumbar extension change **≤+5°**. Wheel travels ~70-90 cm forward.
  - Wheel path straight ahead (lateral wander ≤5 cm). Head neutral, eyes on the floor.
  - Tempo 2-1-2-0 (2 s out, 1 s hold, 2 s back). 6-12 reps.
- **Steps:** 1 Kneel, wheel under the shoulders. 2 Tuck tailbone, roll out. 3 Stop before the back arches. 4 Pull back with the abs.
- **Mistakes:**
  1. *Lower back arches* — at full reach the belly drops, lumbar extension +20°, hips 8 cm below the line. **Metric: lumbar extension change at full reach; good ≤+5°, bad ≥+15°** (or hip-line sag ≥6 cm). Now: the spine takes the load the abs should. Later: the classic ab-wheel back strain. Fix: "Ribs down, tuck, stop earlier."
  2. *Hips stay back* — the hips never open past 110°, only the arms move, wheel travels 30 cm. **Metric: hip angle at full reach; good ≥145°, bad ≤115°.** Now: a shoulder exercise, abs barely loaded (short lever). Later: no progress. Fix: "Hips go forward with the wheel."
  3. *Bending the elbows / pulling with the arms* — elbows flex to 120° to drag the wheel back. **Metric: elbow angle; good ≥165°, bad ≤130°.** Now: triceps and lats do the return. Later: elbow/shoulder strain, abs under-trained. Fix: "Arms straight, pull with the belly."
- **Beginner:** 3 x 5-8 to a half rollout (hip ~130°), lengthen the roll over weeks. Safety: skip with a sore lower back (use the dead bug); kneel on a pad.

## 5. Changelog and open questions

**Changes to the proposed list:** none — all 7 kept. Hanging knee raise is specified on the
captain's chair (the ACE "captain's chair") with the bar version as the harder variant; front plank
gets the long-lever progression (Schoenfeld 2014) instead of longer holds.

**Open questions for the owner**
1. **Cable crunch has no EMG study** and **Pallof press only a 4-person pilot**. Their "parts hit"
   come from anatomy and coaching; the site should not say "studies show" for either.
2. **Upper vs lower abs** is a real disagreement (Lehman & McGill 2001 no difference; Sarti 1996
   and Gomirato 2023 a small bias). Proposed wording: "one muscle; you can bias the lower part a
   little by curling the pelvis, not isolate it."
3. Ekstrom 2007 per-muscle numbers (glute med 72-74%, EO 69%) were read from secondary reviews; the
   abstract only confirms ">45% MVIC for glute med and external oblique in the side bridge".
4. The pelvic-tilt angle for the hanging knee raise (≥15° change) and the ab-wheel lumbar threshold
   (+15°) are coaching cut-offs; no study defines them.
5. Plank hold targets (20-45 s) follow McGill's "short repeated holds" view; the McGill 1999
   absolute norms (seconds) were not retrieved, only the ratios. If the site wants a "your plank
   time vs normal" feature, the paper's tables are needed.
6. Transversus: the site should cue **bracing** (Grenier & McGill 2007), not "pull the navel in",
   even though Richardson's hollowing school disagrees **[DISAGREEMENT]**.
