# Gym real-world logic — checklist for the build team

Research date: 25 Oct 2026. Priority tags: **[P1]** = viewers notice instantly if wrong (breaks
the "real gym" feel), **[P2]** = noticed by anyone who trains, **[P3]** = polish. Sources are
coaching/etiquette references unless a study is named; distances and timings marked
**[OUR VALUE]** are ours, chosen to look right at human scale, not measured.

## A. Arrival and greeting
1. **[P2]** Street → door: normal walking speed 1.2-1.4 m/s; the door opens *before* the person
   reaches it (automatic: starts opening at ~1.5 m; manual: the person slows, reaches with the
   near hand, pulls, steps through, the door closes behind). Nobody walks into a closed door.
2. **[P2]** Inside: 1-2 s pause and look-around at the entrance, then walk to the coach/desk.
   Bag goes on a shelf or the floor by the wall, never carried to a station.
3. **[P1]** Coach greets face to face at 1-1.5 m, not blocking the doorway; asks "which muscle
   today?"; then **walks ahead** to the first station and stops beside it, not on it.

## B. Moving between stations
4. **[P1]** Path-finding: the person walks around every object with ≥ 30 cm clearance; never
   through benches, racks, cables or plates **[OUR VALUE]**. Navmesh must exclude the bar's full
   length on a loaded rack and the cable's travel zone in front of a tower.
5. **[P1]** Never walk between a lifter and the mirror, and never cross directly in front of a
   cable or a bar someone is lifting (StrengthLog etiquette, https://www.strengthlog.com/gym-etiquette).
6. **[P2]** Approach a bench from the side, **turn to face away** from it, then sit: feet land
   ~20 cm in front of the bench edge, hands may touch the bench for the last 10 cm. Nobody sits
   while still facing the bench.
7. **[P2]** Lying back on a bench: sit on the end → hands on the thighs/bench → lower the torso
   in ~1 s while swinging the feet apart to the floor → shuffle 5-10 cm to put the eyes under
   the bar (barbell) or the head on the pad. Getting up: reverse — rock up with a small leg
   swing, sit 0.5 s, stand.
8. **[P2]** Machine/preacher seat: stand beside, adjust first (see D), then sit, then grab the
   handles. Nobody adjusts a seat while sitting on it.
9. **[P3]** Between stations the person carries only what they brought (towel/bottle), walks
   at 1.0-1.2 m/s, slightly slower when tired late in the session.

## C. Dumbbells
10. **[P1]** Taking from the rack: stand square to the rack, feet hip-width, hinge at the hips
    30-45° (knees bent for the low tier, nearly straight for the top tier), take one dumbbell in
    each hand with a neutral grip, stand up with the back flat, then step back. Never pick up with a
    rounded back, never one-handed for anything heavy (Alo Moves trainer tips,
    https://blog.alomoves.com/movement/how-to-pick-up-weights-properly-tips-from-a-trainer).
11. **[P2]** Carry at the sides ("suitcase"), arms straight, dumbbells 5-10 cm from the thighs,
    not swinging past ±15°. Walk to the bench, turn, sit with the dumbbells resting on end on
    the thighs near the knees.
12. **[P1]** Getting heavy dumbbells into a press ("kick-up"): sit with the dumbbells on the
    thighs, palms in; lie back while kicking the knees up one at a time (or both) so the thighs
    throw the dumbbells to the shoulders; the dumbbells arrive at chest level as the back lands
    (Bodybuilding.com dumbbell bench page; NSCA S&C Journal 2000 method quoted by Kiiha,
    https://www.naturalstrength.com/2013/12/how-to-shoulder-heavy-dumbells-by-osmo.html).
    Light dumbbells (≤ 10 kg): just lie back holding them.
13. **[P1]** Finishing a dumbbell press: lower the dumbbells to the chest, bring the knees up to
    meet them, place them on the thighs, rock up to sitting using the leg swing, stand. Never
    drop them beside the bench (shoulder risk and noise; Bodybuilding.com).
14. **[P2]** Returning: carry back, face the rack, hinge, place both in the **same empty slot**
    (handles parallel to the rack), stand, step back. Dumbbells go back where they came from
    (Energie Fitness etiquette).
15. **[P3]** One-arm exercises (concentration curl, kickback): the free dumbbell waits on the
    floor beside the bench, not on the bench.

## D. Barbells, J-hooks and the floor
16. **[P1]** Bench unrack: bar in J-hooks ~5-10 cm above the lifter's straight-arm reach; the
    lifter presses the bar up out of the hooks, then moves it forward ~15-20 cm to over the
    shoulders, 1 s settle, then the first rep. Rerack: press to lockout, move the bar back until
    it touches the uprights, lower into the hooks, let go only when it sits. Coaches call this
    "touch the uprights first" **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**.
17. **[P1]** Squat unrack: hooks at mid-chest; step under, bar on the upper traps, hands just
    outside the shoulders, feet under the bar, stand straight to lift it, let the bar stop
    bobbing (~1 s), then **2-3 steps back**: one step back with the first foot, the second foot
    to width, at most a third small adjustment; never more (elitefts, powerliftingtechnique:
    https://powerliftingtechnique.com/squat-walkout/). Rerack: walk forward until the bar touches
    both uprights, bend the knees to drop it into the hooks.
18. **[P2]** Overhead press from the rack: hooks at upper chest; take the bar in the front rack
    (bar on the collarbones, elbows forward, forearms vertical), stand up with it, 2 small steps
    back, press; rerack by walking in and bending the knees.
19. **[P2]** Lifting a bar from the floor (deadlift, or a curl bar left on the floor): feet
    hip-width under the bar, hinge until the hands reach it, back flat, chest up, push the floor
    away; a curl bar is then held at the thighs. Barbell curls start from this stand-up, not from
    a curl off the floor.
20. **[P2]** Loading plates: slide on from the collar end with both hands, one side then the
    other, collars last; unload in reverse and put plates back on the plate tree. Never leave a
    bar loaded (StrengthLog, Energie etiquette).
21. **[P3]** The EZ bar lives on the barbell rack; it is carried at the thighs with both hands.

## E. Machines and cables
22. **[P1]** Pulley height: stand facing the tower, pull the spring pin at the carriage, slide
    the carriage up/down the rail, release so it clicks into a hole, then clip the attachment to
    the carabiner. Low pulley for curls/overhead rope, top pulley for pushdowns.
23. **[P1]** Weight stack: pull the pin out, push it into a lower/higher plate, release; the
    pin's lanyard is visible. Changing weight happens *between* sets, standing beside the stack.
24. **[P2]** Seat height on a preacher/machine: pull the pop-pin on the seat post, raise/lower,
    release; then sit. Preacher: top of the pad in the armpits.
25. **[P2]** Attachments: unclip after the last set and hang the rope/bar on the attachment
    hooks on the tower, not on the floor.
26. **[P2]** Releasing a cable: step toward the tower first so the stack lands softly (plates
    clack ≈ 1-2 cm drop at most). A stack slamming is the single most "beginner" sound in a gym.

## F. Hygiene and etiquette
27. **[P1]** Wipe the bench/pad after a set block: take the spray bottle or wipe from the wall
    station, spray the towel (not the pad), wipe seat and backrest in 2-3 strokes, return the
    bottle (ACE etiquette, https://www.acefitness.org/resources/everyone/blog/6397/do-you-have-bad-gym-etiquette/).
28. **[P2]** Re-rack everything before leaving a station (dumbbells, plates, attachments).
29. **[P2]** Towel on the bench during use is normal; phone stays in the pocket in the demo.
30. **[P3]** Someone else nearby doing their own thing makes the gym feel real; they never stand
    in the walking path or stare.

## G. Rest periods and set rhythm
31. **[P1]** Rest between sets is visible, not skipped: 60-90 s for isolation (curls, pushdowns,
    kickbacks), 2-3 min for compound lifts (close-grip bench, dips). Schoenfeld 2016: 3 min beat
    1 min for strength and thickness in trained men; Grgic 2017: both viable for beginners.
32. **[P2]** What rest looks like: stand or sit on the bench end, shake the arms out, drink,
    glance at the clock/phone, change the weight if needed. Not frozen, not walking away.
33. **[P2]** First set of each exercise at a lighter load (a "feel" set) for the heavy lifts;
    ramp-up sets in the same exercise are the warm-up (Motricidade 2021 review:
    https://revistas.rcaap.pt/motricidade/article/view/21143).
34. **[P3]** Breathing: inhale on the lowering, exhale on the hard part; visible chest motion.

## H. What the coach does
35. **[P1]** Stands **to the side**, 1.5-2 m away, in the lifter's peripheral vision, never in
    front of the mirror line and never in the bar path. Watches, points, says one cue.
36. **[P1]** Bench press / close-grip bench spot: coach stands **behind the head** of the bench,
    staggered stance, hands under the bar with an alternated grip (one palm up, one down) or
    both palms up inside the lifter's grip, gives the lift-off on a count of three, releases
    slowly, hands hover 5-10 cm under the bar during the set, touches only if the bar stalls
    (elitefts Dave Tate lift-off, https://elitefts.com/education/watch-how-to-properly-do-a-bench-press-lift-off;
    JMU spotting chapter, https://educ.jmu.edu/~strength/KIN_425/kin_425_chapter17_ex_tech_spotting.htm).
37. **[P2]** Dumbbell press spot: at the lifter's **wrists/forearms**, not the elbows, standing
    behind the head; helps the dumbbells up on the kick-up for heavy sets.
38. **[P2]** Dips/curls/pushdowns: no spot. The coach watches from the side and corrects elbow
    position by pointing or a light touch on the elbow.
39. **[P2]** Squat: spotter behind the lifter, hands near the ribs/armpits, steps forward with
    the lifter on the walkout, does not touch unless the lift fails.
40. **[P3]** Coach demos: when showing a mistake, the coach does it with an empty bar or no
    weight and exaggerates it, then shows the fix.

## I. Session ordering rules
41. **[P1]** Order: general warm-up (3-5 min walk/bike or arm circles, optional) → specific
    warm-up sets in the first exercise → **compound before isolation** (close-grip bench before
    pushdowns; dips before kickbacks; barbell curl before concentration curl) → smaller/isolation
    work → core last. ACSM 2009 and Simão 2012 review: large-muscle, multi-joint exercises first
    maximise total load; exercises done early get more reps and larger gains regardless of type
    (https://www.unm.edu/~lkravitz/Article%20folder/ExerciseOrderinRT.html).
42. **[P2]** The muscle the person "chose" goes first in the session (Simão 2012: whatever is
    trained first improves most).
43. **[P2]** Core last because a fatigued trunk makes the heavy lifts less stable
    **[COACHING CONSENSUS]**; direct evidence is thin, so present it as practice, not science.
44. **[P2]** Stretched-position exercises (incline curl, overhead extension) sit in the middle of
    the session, after the heavy lift and before the pump finisher; light finishers end it.
45. **[P3]** Total session for one small muscle group: 9-12 hard sets, 25-35 min including rest;
    the clock on the wall should agree with the rest timers.

## J. Believability ranking (what matters most if time is short)
1. No clipping through equipment; turning before sitting; the kick-up and the dumbbell
   put-down (items 4, 6, 12, 13).
2. Bar leaves and returns to J-hooks correctly; 2-3 step squat walkout (16, 17).
3. Pin, pulley and seat adjustments happen before the set, standing (22-24).
4. Visible rest periods with human behaviour (31, 32).
5. Coach stands to the side; spots the bench from behind the head (35, 36).
6. Wiping the bench and re-racking (27, 28).
Everything else is polish.
