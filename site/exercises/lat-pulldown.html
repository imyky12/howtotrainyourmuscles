How to do the lat pulldown

The lat pulldown is a machine exercise for the lats. It mainly works the lats, with help from the biceps and rear shoulder. Bar to the collarbones, not a trip behind the neck.

Step by step

  1. Let the bar rise 2 sec

    Let the bar rise slowly, about 2 seconds, until the arms are straight.

  2. Arms straight stretch

    Arms straight, shoulder blades lifted. The lats are stretched here.

  3. Pull to collarbones elbows down

    Pull the bar down to your collarbones, elbows driving down and slightly back. Keep the small backward lean the same.

  4. Squeeze elbows by the ribs

    Bar at the collarbones, elbows by your ribs. Squeeze for a moment.

3 common lat pulldown mistakes

1Leaning back

The torso leans back from about 15° to 45° as you pull, and the bar ends at the belly.

Right away
It becomes a half row with momentum; the lats get less of the vertical pull.
Over time
You load more than the lats can handle, and the lower back helps out.
The fix
Lean back a little once, then stay still.

2Behind the neck

The head pushes forward and the bar comes down behind the neck.

Right away
You lift about 15% less (59 vs 70 kg in one study) and the lats don’t work any harder.
Over time
Repeated loaded end-range rotation can irritate the front of the shoulder.
The fix
Bar to the collarbones, chin up.

3Half reps

The elbows stay bent at the top, so the lats never stretch.

Right away
You skip the stretched range.
Over time
Less growth from the same sets.
The fix
Let the arms go straight.

Sets and reps for beginners

3 sets of 8–12. Don’t let the stack slam; guide the bar all the way up before you let go.

Workouts with the lat pulldown

  • Back: No barbell 3 × 8–12, rest 2 min
