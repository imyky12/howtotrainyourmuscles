How to do the cable crossover, low to high

The cable crossover, low to high is a cable exercise for the upper chest. It mainly works the upper chest, with help from the front shoulder.

Watch it in 3D →See the right form and every mistake on a 3D body, with the working muscles lit up Check my form →Your phone camera counts your reps and spots these mistakes

Muscles worked

  • Upper chestmain
  • Front shoulderhelping

Step by step

  1. Let the arms down 2 sec

    Let the handles pull your arms down and back to your sides, slowly.

  2. Stretch hands by the hips

    Hands low and wide by your hips, chest open.

  3. Sweep up and in in a wide arc

    Sweep the hands up and together. The elbows keep the same soft bend.

  4. Squeeze upper-chest height

    Hands meet at upper-chest or chin height. Squeeze for a second.

Setup checklist

  • Pulleys at the lowest setting.
  • Stand tall in a staggered stance, soft elbows.
  • Sweep the hands up and in to meet at upper-chest height.
  • Lower slowly back to your hips.

2 common cable crossover, low to high mistakes

1Curling the handles

The elbows bend and the hands come up to the shoulders, like a biceps curl.

Right away
The biceps do the work, not the chest.
Over time
The upper chest gets little work.
The fix
Keep the elbows softly bent and fixed; sweep the hands up and in.

2Hands finish too high

The hands keep going up above the head.

Right away
It becomes a front raise: the front shoulders take over.
Over time
Shoulder strain and less upper-chest work.
The fix
Stop when the hands meet at upper-chest or chin height.

Sets and reps for beginners

2–3 sets of 12–15. Upper-chest emphasis comes from the direction of the fibres (coaching consensus).

Workouts with the cable crossover, low to high

  • Chest: Machines 2 × 12–15, rest 1–1.5 min
